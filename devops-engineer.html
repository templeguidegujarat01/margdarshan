Overview

What does a DevOps Engineer actually do?

DevOps combines Development and Operations — the role exists to eliminate the friction between writing code and running it reliably in production. A DevOps Engineer automates build, test and deployment pipelines (CI/CD) so software ships fast without breaking, and keeps the underlying infrastructure stable as it scales.

What most guides don't explain: this role has genuinely splintered into three distinguishable tracks by 2026 — traditional DevOps (CI/CD and infrastructure automation), SRE (reliability engineering, on-call, SLO ownership), and Platform Engineering (building internal developer platforms). Each is called "DevOps-adjacent" but involves meaningfully different day-to-day work, and Platform Engineering in particular commands the biggest salary premium of the three, since relatively few engineers in India currently have that specific skill set.

Fit check

Who should aim for a DevOps Engineer career

DevOps is a good fit if you...

  • Enjoy automation and systems thinking — making things run reliably at scale, not just writing features.
  • Want real ownership over how software actually reaches users, not just how it's written.
  • Are willing to build genuine CI/CD pipeline and Kubernetes project experience, since baseline certifications alone no longer differentiate candidates.

Think twice if you...

  • Want to avoid all on-call or production-incident responsibility — the SRE track specifically involves this directly.
  • Expect a single certification like CKA to still be a strong differentiator on its own — it's increasingly just an expected baseline.
  • Want pure software-writing without any infrastructure or ops involvement — the Software Engineer path is the closer match.
Often a transition, not a first job

Routes into a DevOps Engineering career

  • B.Tech in CSE/IT — the most common formal route.
  • BCA — a genuinely viable base, since practical skills matter more than the specific degree.
  • Transition from Software Engineer, Cloud Engineer or Sysadmin — a genuinely common, valid path; many DevOps engineers move in after gaining hands-on production experience elsewhere.

Career growth paths

Moving from a plain DevOps Engineer role into SRE or Cloud Architect after 5-6 years is reported as one of the fastest ways to push salary past the mid-level range — these adjacent, more specialised paths are in high demand and pay a genuine premium. Higher-level certifications — AWS DevOps Professional, Azure Expert-level — add real weight, but a further academic degree isn't the typical growth lever here; deliberate track and skill choices matter more.

Honest take

Advantages and disadvantages

Advantages

  • One of the most reliably well-paid tech specialisations, since genuinely reliable, scalable, cost-efficient systems require scarce skills.
  • Genuine track diversity — traditional DevOps, SRE, Platform Engineering — lets you pick the flavour matching your strengths.
  • Strong global remote opportunity for skilled engineers, reported to reach a meaningful premium over domestic-only roles.

Disadvantages

  • The SRE track specifically comes with real on-call and incident-response responsibility, which isn't for everyone.
  • A once-differentiating certification like CKA is now closer to a baseline expectation at mid-senior levels — real project depth matters more.
  • Platform Engineering, the highest-paid track, requires genuinely scarce skills that take real time and effort to build.

Common myths

Myths vs facts

Myth: DevOps is just "Cloud Engineering with CI/CD added on."

Fact: While the two overlap, DevOps specifically centres on automating the software delivery pipeline itself, and by 2026 has split into three genuinely distinct career tracks — traditional DevOps, SRE and Platform Engineering — each with different day-to-day work and pay.

Myth: Getting a Kubernetes (CKA) certification guarantees you stand out.

Fact: CKA is reported to have become a baseline filter rather than a differentiator at mid-senior levels in 2026 — real, demonstrable pipeline and Kubernetes project experience now matters more than the certificate alone.

Myth: DevOps engineers just write scripts all day.

Fact: The field has genuinely diversified — SRE-track engineers own production reliability and incident response, while Platform Engineers build the internal tools other engineering teams depend on. "Writing scripts" barely captures either.
