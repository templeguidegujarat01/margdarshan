Software Engineer — Career Guide & Roadmap

11 min read · A role, not a single degree · Reachable via CSE, BCA, or a strong self-taught portfolio

"Software Engineer" isn't a degree you enroll in — it's a role you can reach through CSE, BCA, or increasingly through a strong self-taught portfolio. And the number that decides your salary the most isn't your college or your years of experience — it's whether you land at a services company or a product company, a gap that's reported to be widening, not narrowing, in 2026. This guide explains exactly how that works.

Overview

What does a Software Engineer actually do?

A Software Engineer designs, builds, tests and maintains software — websites, mobile apps, backend systems and the large-scale platforms behind products used by millions. It's a role, not a single fixed degree: engineers reach it through a B.Tech in CSE/IT, a BCA, or increasingly through self-taught skill-building with a strong project portfolio, since many companies now hire primarily on demonstrated ability.

India's software engineering job market in 2026 is the largest it has ever been by headcount, and the layoffs of 2022-23 at global tech companies have largely been absorbed, with hiring resuming across most segments. What's changed is how differentiated the outcomes are — company type now predicts your salary more than almost anything else.

Fit check

Who should aim for a Software Engineer career

This path is a good fit if you...

  • Genuinely enjoy building things and are willing to practice algorithmic problem-solving (DSA) seriously, not just once.
  • Want merit-based, fast salary growth through job switches rather than slow, tenure-based increments alone.
  • Are comfortable proving your skills again at every interview, even years into your career.

Think twice if you...

  • Want steady, predictable growth without ever needing to prove yourself again through interviews.
  • Strongly dislike algorithmic interview prep — DSA is a genuine, sometimes frustrating barrier at product companies.
  • Assume your college brand alone will matter more than what you can actually build and explain in an interview.
Multiple valid entry points

Routes into a Software Engineering career

  • B.Tech in CSE/IT — the most common and best-recognised route, via JEE, requiring PCM at Class 12.
  • BCA or BSc Computer Science — open to any stream at Class 12, no JEE required.
  • Self-taught + strong portfolio — increasingly viable, especially at startups and through skill-based hiring, provided you can demonstrate real coding ability and completed projects.

Step-by-step

Your path to becoming a Software Engineer

  1. 1

    Learn programming fundamentals

    Python, Java or C++ — pick one and get genuinely comfortable with it.

  2. 2

    Practice DSA seriously

    This is the primary technical filter — aim for consistent, not just occasional, practice.

  3. 3

    Learn a stack and build real projects

    Frontend, backend or full-stack — ship 3-4 genuine projects, not tutorials copied line by line.

  4. 4

    Land your first job

    Often a services company as a starting point — that's normal, not a failure.

  5. 5

    Make the service-to-product switch

    Typically in year 2-3 — described as the single biggest salary lever in Indian tech.

The interview bar varies by company type

How hiring actually works

Services companies (TCS, Infosys, Wipro)Mass campus hiring, a basic coding test, and an HR round — the DSA bar is generally lower than at product companies.
Product companies & unicornsMultiple DSA rounds plus system design for senior roles; portfolio and GitHub activity genuinely matter.
Self-taught / portfolio routeA take-home assignment or project review plus technical interviews — your degree is scrutinised far less than your actual work.
Typical processOnline coding assessment → technical interview(s) on DSA/domain skills → system design round (for senior roles) → HR/culture-fit round → offer.
Cost varies a lot by route

Cost of each route

A B.Tech CSE/IT degree commonly costs ₹2-8 lakh at government institutes and ₹5-20 lakh at private colleges over 4 years (see the CSE page for details). A BCA is generally more affordable, often ₹30,000-1,50,000 per year (see the BCA page). Structured coding bootcamps, for students choosing the self-taught-plus-guided-programme route, commonly cost roughly ₹50,000-3,00,000 depending on the format and duration — always verify a bootcamp's actual placement outcomes and reviews carefully before enrolling, since quality varies widely across providers.

The number that actually matters most: company type

Software Engineer salary in India

This is the field's central honest truth — company type predicts your salary more than years of experience or college brand. These are broad, commonly reported ranges, not guarantees.

Fresher, product companies/startups₹6–12 LPA

The gap is reported to be widening, not narrowing, with experience — company type dominates the outcome.

Beyond the first job

Career growth paths

Beyond the services-to-product switch, the clearest growth levers are specialisation — AI/ML engineering currently commands a real, verified salary premium, alongside distributed systems, cloud/platform engineering and deep system design expertise. Some engineers move into an engineering management track over time, while others pursue an MS abroad for research-oriented or specialised technical roles. Reaching senior levels (SDE-2/3, Staff Engineer) consistently combines strong system design ability with genuine ownership of features or systems, not just tenure.

Honest take

Advantages and disadvantages

Advantages

  • Genuinely merit-based salary growth — switching companies with proven skills often beats years of tenure alone.
  • Multiple valid entry routes — a degree isn't the only door in, if you can build and prove real skills.
  • Genuinely global remote opportunities, and a real, currently persisting premium for AI-adjacent skills.

Disadvantages

  • The DSA-heavy interview process is a genuine, often frustrating barrier — even for experienced engineers switching jobs.
  • The services-vs-product pay gap is wide and doesn't close with tenure alone — proactive job-switching is often necessary.
  • The 2022-23 layoff wave showed the field isn't recession-proof, even though hiring has since recovered.

Common myths

Myths vs facts

Myth: A CS degree from a top college guarantees a high starting salary.

Fact: Company type (services vs product) is reported as a bigger predictor of salary than college brand or years of experience — the degree and college matter less than most candidates think.

Myth: Once you're a software engineer, your salary grows steadily with tenure alone.

Fact: Real salary growth in Indian tech mostly comes from switching from a services company to a product company early in your career — staying at one company on annual increments alone grows much more slowly.

Myth: You must have a Computer Science degree to become a software engineer.

Fact: BCA, BSc, and increasingly self-taught engineers with a strong project portfolio are hired directly, especially at startups and skill-focused hiring pipelines — a portfolio can matter more than the degree name.
