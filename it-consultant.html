IT Consultant — Career Guide & Roadmap

10 min read · A bridge between business and technology · Firm tier matters more than almost any specific skill

Overview

What does an IT Consultant actually do?

An IT Consultant is a bridge between business problems and technology solutions — analysing what a company actually needs, recommending software, cloud, data or digital solutions, and helping guide implementation. It's a genuinely different day-to-day from a Software Engineer: less hands-on coding, considerably more client communication, requirement analysis and business-facing problem solving.

What most guides don't explain clearly is how much which firm hires you changes this career. An entry-level Analyst role at an IT services company like TCS pays and feels very different from a Technology Consultant role at Accenture, which differs again from a Big 4 or MBB-style strategy consulting arm. Firm tier is arguably the single biggest lever in this career — more than almost any specific technical skill.

Fit check

Who should aim for an IT Consultant career

IT Consulting is a good fit if you...

  • Enjoy explaining technical ideas in plain business language, more than writing code all day.
  • Want variety — different clients, industries and projects — over deep specialisation in one codebase.
  • Are comfortable with travel and client-facing work, and want a path that could eventually lead toward an MBA or strategy leadership.

Think twice if you...

  • Want deep, hands-on technical work above all else — Software Engineer or Cloud Engineer are the closer match.
  • Dislike travel or constant client-facing communication — a genuine, regular part of this role at most firms.
  • Assume the "IT Consultant" title alone guarantees the ₹20-28 LPA figures quoted for Accenture/McKinsey-tier roles — those describe specific firms and experience levels.
Multiple valid entry points

Routes into an IT Consulting career

  • B.Tech or BCA — the most common route, via campus placement into an IT services company's consulting arm.
  • An MBA (especially from a top B-school) — significantly improves entry into Big 4/MBB-style consulting specifically, and generally means a higher starting package.
  • Lateral entry after 2-4 years as a software engineer or analyst — a genuinely common route into consulting, bringing real technical credibility.

Firm tier decides more than almost anything else

How hiring actually works

Big 4 firms (Deloitte, Accenture, EY, KPMG, PwC)Campus and lateral hiring, with case-study and behavioural interviews; meaningfully higher pay and more diverse international client exposure.

An MBA is the one genuinely large added cost

Cost of each route

A B.Tech in CSE commonly costs ₹2-8 lakh at government institutes and ₹5-20 lakh at private colleges (see the CSE page), while a BCA is generally more affordable at ₹30,000-1,50,000 per year (see the BCA page). If you're specifically targeting Big 4 or MBB-tier consulting, an MBA is a real, substantial added investment — often 2 years and ₹10-30 lakh or more at a top B-school — worth weighing carefully against the direct-entry route into IT services consulting, which requires no such additional cost.

The MBA lever, and certifications as a lighter alternative

Career growth paths

An MBA, especially from a top B-school, is the genuine, common lever specifically for moving into Big 4 or MBB-tier consulting, or accelerating into leadership roles faster than the standard track. For those who don't want that time and cost commitment, certifications like PMP, ITIL, SAP or Salesforce add real weight to a candidacy without requiring a full postgraduate degree. Many consultants also eventually move toward entrepreneurship or in-house strategy roles at the companies they once advised.

Honest take

Advantages and disadvantages

Advantages

  • A genuine mix of business and technology exposure, appealing if you don't want to choose purely one or the other.
  • Real variety — different clients, industries and projects rather than one codebase for years.
  • A strong path toward leadership, strategy roles, or an eventual MBA and entrepreneurship.

Disadvantages

  • "IT Consultant" salary claims online vary enormously by firm tier — a headline Accenture/McKinsey figure isn't representative of most entry-level roles.
  • Often involves real travel and onsite client work, which isn't for everyone.
  • The highest-paying tier (MBB-style strategy consulting) is genuinely difficult to enter without a top-tier MBA or exceptional engineering pedigree.

Common myths

Myths vs facts

Myth: Every "IT Consultant" job pays like the Accenture/McKinsey figures you see quoted online.

Fact: Those numbers describe specific firm tiers at specific experience levels, often 5+ years. The far larger number of entry-level IT Consultant/IT Analyst roles at IT services companies start at a much more modest ₹3.5-8 LPA.

Myth: You need an MBA to become an IT Consultant.

Fact: An MBA meaningfully helps for Big 4/MBB-tier consulting specifically, but most IT Consultant roles, especially at IT services companies, are entered directly via a B.Tech/BCA campus placement without one.

Myth: IT Consulting is basically the same job as Software Engineering with a different title.

Fact: It's genuinely more business-and-client-facing — less hands-on coding, more requirement analysis, client communication and case-study-style problem solving. The skill set and daily work differ meaningfully from a pure engineering role.
