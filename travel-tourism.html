Overview

What is Travel & Tourism?

Travel & Tourism (typically a Bachelor of Arts (BA) or Bachelor of Business Administration (BBA) in Tourism/Travel Management) trains students in the business side of travel — planning itineraries and tour packages, managing tour operations, destination marketing, and understanding the broader travel and hospitality ecosystem, including airlines, transport and local tourism boards. It's distinct from Hotel Management, which focuses specifically on running a hotel property.

It's offered at institutions like the Indian Institute of Tourism and Travel Management (IITTM), several university tourism departments, and private colleges offering BBA in Tourism & Travel Management, often with a strong practical/internship component.

Fit check

Who should choose Travel & Tourism

Travel & Tourism is a good fit if you...

  • Genuinely enjoy planning trips, researching destinations, and organising complex logistics.
  • Are comfortable with a service and sales-oriented career, especially in client-facing roles.
  • Are open to adapting to travel-tech and digital booking platforms as a growing part of the industry.

Think twice if you...

  • Want hands-on hotel operations specifically — Bachelor of Hotel Management (BHM) is the more direct route for that.
  • Expect the field to be untouched by online booking trends — significant parts of simple travel booking have shifted online.
  • Dislike client-facing sales and consultation work — much of the entry-level role involves this directly.
Eligibility

Who can apply for Travel & Tourism

  • Class 12 pass (10+2) from any stream, typically with a minimum aggregate around 45-50%, varying by institute.
  • No specific Class 12 subject is compulsory, since it's a service and management-oriented field.
  • Institutes like IITTM may conduct their own entrance test or admit through national management entrance scores, depending on the specific programme.

Fees & scholarships

Fees at central/government institutes like IITTM are typically in the range of ₹1-2 lakh per year. Private colleges offering BBA in Tourism Management can range from ₹50,000-2 lakh per year. Merit-based scholarships are available at select institutes. Always confirm current fees directly on the official institute website.

Honest take

Advantages and disadvantages

Advantages

  • Growing travel-tech sector alongside traditional tourism roles offers newer, evolving career options.
  • Genuinely varied work — destination research, client consultation, operations and marketing.
  • India's growing domestic and inbound tourism sector provides substantial, ongoing demand.

Disadvantages

  • Online booking platforms have reduced demand for some traditional, simple travel-agent roles.
  • Entry-level pay can be modest, with income growth often tied to sales performance and experience.
  • The industry is sensitive to external shocks — economic downturns, travel restrictions or global events can significantly affect demand.

Common myths

Myths vs facts

Myth: Travel & Tourism and Hotel Management are the same field.

Fact: They're related but distinct — Travel & Tourism focuses on trip planning, tour operations and destinations, while Hotel Management focuses on running a hotel property specifically.

Myth: Online booking sites have made travel professionals obsolete.

Fact: Simple bookings have shifted online, but complex itinerary planning, corporate travel management and specialised trip consulting still rely on skilled travel professionals.

Myth: This career means you get to travel constantly for free.

Fact: Most roles are office/client-facing rather than travel-heavy, and while some travel perks or familiarisation trips exist in certain roles, the day-to-day job is largely planning and managing travel for others.
