Overview

What is Cyber Security?

  • Cyber Security is the practice of protecting computers, networks, applications and data from unauthorised access, attacks and breaches.
  • It spans several genuinely distinct specialisations — network security, application security, cloud security, ethical hacking/penetration testing, digital forensics, and governance, risk & compliance (GRC) — each with its own skill set and salary curve.
  • As banks, hospitals, government departments and every digital business move more of their operations online, the need for people who can defend those systems has grown sharply — and unlike many "trending" tech fields, this one is backed by a documented, quantified skills gap rather than social-media buzz.
Fit check

Who should choose Cyber Security

✔ Ideal for you if…

  • Genuinely enjoy a puzzle-solving, "how would I break this" mindset.
  • Want a field with a genuine, quantified long-term demand story, not just hype.
  • Are willing to continuously earn certifications (Security+, CEH, OSCP) — they matter as much as the degree here.

✖ Reconsider if…

  • Expect the degree alone, with no certifications or hands-on practice, to be enough for a strong starting salary.
  • Want a completely predictable 9-to-5 — active security incidents can mean on-call or after-hours response work.
  • Dislike ongoing self-learning — attack techniques and defences evolve constantly, and staying still means falling behind.
Eligibility

Who can enter Cyber Security

  • ✓B.Tech Cyber Security Engineering route: Class 12 pass with Physics, Chemistry and Mathematics (PCM), admitted via Joint Entrance Examination Main (JEE Main), a state CET or a university-specific test.
  • ✓BCA with a Cyber Security specialisation: Class 12 pass in any stream, generally admitted via Common University Entrance Test (CUET) or Class 12 merit — no PCM requirement.
  • ✓Any-degree + certification route: Many entry-level SOC/analyst roles accept graduates from any stream, provided you hold a recognised certification like CompTIA Security+ or CEH.

Degree fees plus ongoing certification costs

Fees & certification costs

For the B.Tech route, government-funded institutes remain the most economical: total fees for the full 4-year programme commonly fall in the ₹2-8 lakh range, with private colleges typically costing ₹5-15 lakh total. What's different about this field is the real, recurring cost of certifications on top of your degree — CompTIA Security+ is relatively affordable, while CEH training and exam costs commonly run ₹65,000-75,000 through EC-Council approved centres, and advanced certifications like OSCP or CISSP add further cost. Budget for certifications as a genuine, ongoing part of this career, not a one-time expense.

The certification ladder

Certifications & higher studies

Certifications function almost like a career ladder in this field: start with CompTIA Security+ as your foundational credential, then move to CEH for offensive security or government/defence-linked roles. For advanced penetration testing, OSCP is the recognised gold standard, while CISSP and CISM mark the shift toward security management and governance roles. An M.Tech in Cyber Security via Graduate Aptitude Test in Engineering (GATE) or an MS abroad suits students aiming at research or global security leadership roles.

Honest take

Advantages and disadvantages

Advantages

  • A genuine, quantified talent gap (NASSCOM: ~1.5 million professionals needed vs ~80,000 qualified), not marketing hype.
  • Genuinely multiple valid entry points — B.Tech, BCA, or any degree plus certification — not locked to one path.
  • Strong remote and global opportunity, including remote roles paying at international rates for skilled engineers.

Disadvantages

  • A plain degree without certifications or hands-on practice is a genuinely weaker candidate profile with modest starting pay.
  • The highest-paying track (offensive security/pentesting) is also the most technically demanding to break into.
  • Certifications add real, recurring cost — CEH alone commonly runs ₹65,000-75,000.
Common myths

Myths vs facts

MYTH A cyber security degree alone guarantees a well-paying job. ↓
FACT

Entry salary depends heavily on certifications and hands-on proof of skill (CTFs, labs) — a plain degree without either starts modestly, around ₹3.5-5 LPA, while the same graduate with CEH or Security+ can start meaningfully higher.

MYTH You need an engineering degree to work in cyber security. ↓
FACT

Many entry-level SOC/security analyst roles accept graduates from any stream — BCA, B.Sc, even Commerce or Arts — provided they hold a recognised certification like CompTIA Security+ or CEH.

MYTH Cyber security demand in India is more hype than reality. ↓
FACT

NASSCOM estimates India needs about 1.5 million cybersecurity professionals against roughly 80,000 currently qualified — a documented, large skills gap, not a marketing claim.
