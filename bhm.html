Overview

What is BHM?

BHM (Bachelor of Hotel Management) is a 4-year professional degree training students to manage hotel operations across front office, food & beverage service, food production, and housekeeping, alongside hospitality business fundamentals like finance and marketing. It's a genuinely practical, hands-on programme, with substantial time spent in training kitchens, mock front desks and industrial training placements at real hotels.

The 21 central Institutes of Hotel Management (IHMs), including IHM Delhi (Pusa) and IHM Mumbai, are the most respected destinations, admitted through the national NCHM JEE entrance exam, alongside private institutes and university-affiliated hotel management colleges.

Fit check

Who should choose BHM

BHM is a good fit if you...

  • Genuinely enjoy service, hospitality and creating a good guest experience.
  • Are comfortable with a hands-on, physically active degree, and later, rotational shift work including nights and weekends.
  • Are drawn to hospitality's genuinely global career mobility, including opportunities to work abroad.

Think twice if you...

  • Want fixed, predictable 9-to-5 hours from the start — hospitality operations run continuously, and shift work is standard early on.
  • Expect management-level pay immediately — entry-level roles are genuinely service-floor and operational.
  • Dislike sustained interpersonal, guest-facing interaction — that's the core of most entry-level hospitality roles.
Eligibility

Who can apply for BHM

  • Class 12 pass (10+2) from any stream, with English as a compulsory subject, typically requiring around 50% aggregate.
  • NCHM JEE has an upper age limit for central IHM admission, typically around 22 years for General category, with relaxations for reserved categories — check the current year's notification.
  • No specific Class 12 subject beyond a strong grasp of English is compulsory.

The main entrance route

NCHM JEE — exam details

Conducting authorityNational Testing Agency (NTA), on behalf of the National Council for Hotel Management and Catering Technology (NCHMCT).
Who takes itClass 12 students (any stream) seeking admission to the 21 central IHMs and several affiliated state/private institutes.
Exam sectionsNumerical Ability & Analytical Aptitude, Reasoning & Logical Deduction, General Knowledge & Current Affairs, English Language, and Aptitude for Service Sector.
After qualifyingCounselling rounds based on rank, seat allotment across IHMs, document verification and fee payment.
Affordable at central IHMs

Fees & scholarships

Central IHM fees are typically in the range of ₹80,000-1.5 lakh per year, being government-supported institutions. Private hotel management colleges can charge considerably more, often ₹1.5-3.5 lakh per year. Merit-based and need-based scholarships are available at several IHMs. Always confirm current fees directly on the official NCHMCT or institute website.

Honest take

Advantages and disadvantages

Advantages

  • Strong campus placement tradition at central IHMs, with major hotel chains recruiting directly.
  • Genuine international career mobility — hospitality skills transfer well to jobs abroad.
  • Diverse outcomes beyond hotels — aviation, cruise lines, events and healthcare administration.

Disadvantages

  • Entry-level roles genuinely involve rotational shifts, including nights, weekends and holidays.
  • Entry-level pay at smaller hotels can be modest relative to the demanding, physically active workload.
  • Career growth to senior management roles takes sustained years of operational experience.

Common myths

Myths vs facts

Myth: BHM only leads to becoming a hotel receptionist.

Fact: Graduates work across front office, F&B, housekeeping and eventually hotel management, alongside genuinely different industries like aviation, cruise lines, events and healthcare administration.

Myth: Hospitality careers always mean low pay.

Fact: While entry-level pay is modest, senior hospitality roles — department heads and General Managers at established hotel chains — can pay very well, especially with international experience.

Myth: You need cooking skills or a culinary background specifically to study BHM.

Fact: BHM covers food production as one rotation among several — front office, housekeeping and F&B service are equally core, so no specific prior culinary skill is required.
