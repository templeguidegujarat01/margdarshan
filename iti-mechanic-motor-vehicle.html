Overview

What is the Mechanic Motor Vehicle trade?

  • ITI Mechanic Motor Vehicle is a 2-year, NCVT/DGT-affiliated trade under the Craftsman Training Scheme, training students to diagnose, service and repair cars and light vehicles — engine systems, transmission, braking, steering, suspension and vehicle electrical systems.
  • It's one of the most practically relevant ITI trades given India's rapidly growing vehicle ownership.
  • Training is heavily hands-on, working on real vehicle systems and components, combined with trade theory and diagnostic fundamentals, following the standard Craftsmen Training Scheme (CTS) structure.
Read more
  • Many students also pursue additional brand-specific or EV-focused training after this trade to stay current with evolving vehicle technology.
Fit check

Who should choose this trade

✔ Ideal for you if…

  • Are genuinely interested in how vehicles work and enjoy diagnosing mechanical problems.
  • Want strong, steady demand given India's large and growing vehicle fleet.
  • Are interested in eventually running your own garage or service business.

✖ Reconsider if…

  • Specifically want to work on heavy trucks and diesel machinery — Mechanic Diesel fits better.
  • Are uncomfortable with physically demanding, often dirty hands-on repair work.
  • Expect the base trade alone to cover full Electric Vehicle (EV) systems — that typically needs additional training.

Beyond this trade

Higher studies & progression

Many institutes allow lateral entry into Diploma in Automobile Engineering for ITI Mechanic Motor Vehicle holders, typically into a later year, though rules vary by state and institute. Given the growing EV market, additional EV technician certifications are an increasingly valuable next step, since the base trade centres on conventional petrol/diesel systems. Brand-specific training programmes offered by car manufacturers after hiring are also a common and practical way to specialise further.

Honest take

Advantages and disadvantages

Advantages

  • Strong, steady demand given India's large and growing vehicle ownership.
  • Genuine self-employment potential through starting your own garage.
  • Widely recognised trade with straightforward dealership/service centre hiring pathways.

Disadvantages

  • Physically demanding, often dirty hands-on repair work.
  • Base trade doesn't fully cover EV systems — requires additional training to stay current.
  • Entry-level private-sector pay can be modest without apprenticeship experience.
