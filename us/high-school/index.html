How GPA works

{{ G.admissions.gpa.text }}

In practice: an A in a hard class counts for more than a number on your transcript suggests, and a weighted 4.3 from one school is not automatically "better" than an unweighted 3.9 from another. Colleges see your school's profile and course offerings alongside your grades.

Learn from the data

Common mistakes in high school

{% include phone-fold.html id="mistakes" %}
  • Avoiding hard classes to protect GPA. Strength of curriculum mattered considerably to {{ F_curr.pct }}% of colleges in NACAC's survey. Challenge yourself where you can still earn good grades.
  • Treating one test as everything. Grades in college-prep courses ({{ F_prep.pct }}%) far outweigh test scores ({{ F_test.pct }}%) on average — but check the colleges you want, because some require tests.
  • Applying Early Decision without checking money. ED is binding, so you cannot compare financial aid offers.
  • Assuming one state's rules apply everywhere. Required courses and minimum GPAs differ by state and by university system.
  • Missing aid deadlines. The FAFSA opens by October 1 of senior year, and many states and colleges have earlier deadlines than the federal one.
