BA Political Science — Understanding Power, Government & the State

9 min read · UGC-recognised undergraduate degree · Open after Class 12, any stream

BA Political Science studies how governments are formed, how power is exercised and checked, how the Indian Constitution works, and how countries interact on the world stage. It's the single most closely aligned degree with the UPSC Civil Services syllabus, but it also feeds directly into law, journalism, policy research and international affairs. This guide covers exactly what the degree involves and where it leads.

Overview

What is BA Political Science?

BA Political Science is a 3-year undergraduate degree (4 years for Honours with Research under NEP 2020) that studies political theory, the structure and working of the Indian government and Constitution, comparative politics across countries, public administration, and international relations. It trains students to analyse institutions, ideologies and current affairs critically, rather than simply describing how governments are supposed to work on paper.

It's offered widely across Indian universities. Delhi University colleges — Hindu College, Lady Shri Ram College, Hansraj College and Kirori Mal College — are among the most competitive destinations, alongside Jawaharlal Nehru University's undergraduate programmes, Presidency University Kolkata, and Jamia Millia Islamia, under the UGC framework.

Fit check

Who should choose BA Political Science

BA Political Science is a good fit if you...

  • Follow current affairs, government policy and news with genuine curiosity, not just for exams.
  • Are seriously considering UPSC Civil Services, law, journalism, policy research or diplomacy.
  • Enjoy debate and argument, and are comfortable engaging with contested, sometimes politically sensitive topics objectively.

Think twice if you...

  • Have no interest in reading political theory or current governance issues regularly.
  • Want a degree with a fixed, direct pipeline into campus placements straight after graduation.
  • Are only choosing it as a "safe" degree without genuinely wanting a career it leads toward — the payoff usually comes from what you build on top of it.
Eligibility

Who can apply for BA Political Science

  • Class 12 pass (10+2) from a recognised board, typically 45-50% aggregate for General/EWS/OBC and slightly relaxed for SC/ST/PwD categories, varying by university.
  • Open to students from any stream — Arts, Commerce or Science — since no specific Class 12 subject is compulsory.
  • Having studied Political Science or Civics at Class 11-12 helps with familiarity but is not a strict requirement at most colleges.

Very affordable at government colleges

Fees & scholarships

BA Political Science is one of the most affordable degrees in India at government-funded colleges — total fees for the full 3-year course at a DU or central university college are typically in the range of ₹15,000-60,000, well below professional or private-college courses. Private and deemed universities charge considerably more, often ₹1-3 lakh per year. Merit-based, SC/ST and income-linked scholarships are available at most government institutions. Always confirm current fees directly on your target college's official admission page.

Beyond the degree

Higher studies

An MA in Political Science or International Relations — at institutions like Jawaharlal Nehru University, Delhi University or the Jindal School of International Affairs — is the natural next step for students aiming at research, teaching (via UGC-NET) or diplomacy-adjacent careers. Others follow with a 3-year LLB for a law career, an MBA for a corporate/policy-consulting route, or move directly into UPSC/state PSC preparation, all of which build well on the degree's foundation in governance and institutions.

Honest take

Advantages and disadvantages

Advantages

  • The degree most directly aligned with the UPSC Civil Services syllabus.
  • Extremely affordable at government colleges relative to professional or private courses.
  • Flexible base for multiple follow-on paths — law, journalism, policy, diplomacy — not locked into one outcome.

Disadvantages

  • No direct campus placement pipeline the way engineering or CA-linked degrees have.
  • Strong outcomes generally need a further exam, professional course or master's degree.
  • Engaging seriously with political topics requires staying balanced and objective, which some students find challenging.

Common myths

Myths vs facts

Myth: BA Political Science is only useful if you become a politician.

Fact: Very few graduates enter electoral politics directly — most build careers in civil services, law, policy, journalism, media or NGOs, using the degree's grounding in governance and institutions.

Myth: You need to have studied Political Science in Class 11-12 to take the degree.

Fact: Most universities admit students from any Class 12 stream and subject combination — prior study helps but isn't a strict requirement.

Myth: It's not a "real" analytical subject compared to Economics or Science.

Fact: Political Science involves rigorous analysis of institutions, ideologies, comparative systems and policy outcomes — it is assessed and taught with the same academic seriousness as any other social science.
