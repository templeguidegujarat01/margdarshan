Eligibility

Who can apply for Robotics Engineering

  • Class 12 pass with Physics, Chemistry and Mathematics (PCM) from a recognised board.
  • Minimum aggregate typically 50-60% at most colleges offering a dedicated Robotics programme — generally more flexible than IIT/NIT cutoffs for core branches.
  • Alternatively, standard PCM eligibility for Mechanical, Electronics or Computer Science if you choose the specialise-later route instead.
Step-by-step

Your path through Robotics Engineering

  1. 1

    Class 11–12, PCM

    Build a strong Physics and Maths base, and start basic programming if possible.

  2. 2

    Choose your route

  3. 3

    4-year B.Tech/B.E.

  4. 4

ROS, Python, C++ and MATLAB are the core tools across almost every robotics role — hands-on comfort with these, built through real projects, is reported as one of the clearest differentiators recruiters look for in this field.

Wide range by institute type

Fees & scholarships

Government-funded institutes offering Robotics or Mechatronics-adjacent programmes remain the most economical route: total fees for the full 4-year programme commonly fall in the ₹2-8 lakh range, with fee waivers available based on family income and category. Private colleges vary considerably — total costs of roughly ₹6-20 lakh are typical, since many Robotics-specific programmes sit at newer, often private institutions. Merit-based scholarships are available at some institutes; always confirm current fees directly on your target college's official admission page.

Beyond the degree

Higher studies

An M.Tech in Robotics or Mechatronics via Graduate Aptitude Test in Engineering (GATE) is the standard route into IITs/NITs for research and specialised industrial roles. An MS abroad — at globally recognised robotics programmes like Carnegie Mellon or Georgia Tech — is common for students targeting advanced research or global robotics companies. Focused certifications in ROS, PLC/SCADA or AI/ML for robotics can meaningfully speed up entry into better-paid specialised roles without a full postgraduate degree.

Good to know

Frequently asked questions

For most students, going to a stronger Mechanical, Electronics or Computer Science college and specialising in robotics through electives, projects and certifications is the safer route, since dedicated Robotics degrees are still newer and less standardised in India, with fewer colleges offering them and less established recruiter familiarity. A dedicated Robotics degree can make sense at a genuinely strong, well-established programme with real industry tie-ups — check placement records carefully rather than assuming the specific degree name matters most.
Reported figures vary widely by source, college and role. The median robotics engineer salary in India is commonly reported around ₹6-8 LPA, with a broader range of roughly ₹3-19 LPA depending on the college and placement record. Experienced professionals with genuine AI/ML specialisation are reported to earn ₹20-25 LPA — a meaningfully higher, skills-dependent outcome, not a fresher's starting point.
It depends on the college. Some institutes admit through Joint Entrance Examination Main (JEE Main), others through a state-level CET, and several private universities run their own entrance test, such as CUCET for Chandigarh University. There isn't a single dedicated national entrance exam specifically for Robotics the way JEE Advanced exists for core Indian Institute of Technology (IIT) branches.
Most IITs don't offer a standalone undergraduate Robotics branch — students interested in robotics typically join Mechanical or Electronics Engineering and specialise through electives, minors, and research projects. A small number of newer institutes, including some interdisciplinary programmes, have begun offering more dedicated robotics-focused tracks.
Beyond the core coursework, hands-on skills reported as most valued by recruiters include Python and C++ programming, ROS (Robot Operating System), MATLAB, PLC programming, CAD/mechanical design, sensor integration (LiDAR, computer vision, ultrasonic), embedded systems work with Arduino or Raspberry Pi, and foundational AI/machine learning for robotic perception. A genuine project portfolio matters more here than in most engineering branches.
