Company Secretary (CS)

7 min read · Regulated by ICSI · Open after Class 12, any stream

A Company Secretary is a company's in-house legal and governance expert — the person a board relies on to stay compliant, file the right documents on time, and get regulatory decisions right. Certified by the Institute of Company Secretaries of India (ICSI), it's one of India's most affordable professional qualifications relative to its responsibility.

Overview

What is a Company Secretary?

  • A Company Secretary is a governance and compliance professional certified by the ICSI to advise company boards, ensure compliance with the Companies Act, SEBI regulations and other corporate laws, and act as the legal bridge between a company, its board, and its shareholders.
  • Like CA, it's a professional qualification earned directly through ICSI, not a university degree.
  • Every listed company and many large private companies in India are legally required to appoint a full-time Company Secretary, which keeps demand for the profession steady.
Read moreRead less↓
  • CS professionals sign off on statutory filings, draft board resolutions and agreements, and are increasingly involved in Environmental, Social and Governance (ESG) reporting, M&A and insolvency-related work.
  • Many move into legal counsel, compliance leadership or independent practice.
Eligibility

Who should choose CA, and when to start

CS suits students who enjoy reading and interpreting law, are meticulous about documentation and deadlines, and want a respected governance qualification without full-time university attendance.

After Class 10

  • There's no direct CS registration at this stage — focus on choosing a Class 11-12 stream first.
  • Commerce with Business Studies helps, but Science and Arts students take up CS successfully too.

After Class 12

  • Register for the CSEET (CS Executive Entrance Test) — open to students from any stream, with no minimum percentage requirement.
  • Graduates (50%+) and postgraduates can skip CSEET via Direct Entry into the Executive Programme, after paying a CSEET exemption fee.
One entrance test, then sequential levels

How CS admission actually works

You register directly with ICSI and progress through CSEET, Executive and Professional in sequence, alongside mandatory training.

How CS admission actually works

StepWhat happens
1. CSEETRegister and clear the CS Executive Entrance Test after Class 12 (graduates can use Direct Entry instead).
2. Executive ProgrammeA roughly 9-month programme with two modules covering company law, tax and business laws.
3. Training programmesComplete ICSI's mandatory orientation and skill-development programmes between levels.
4. Professional ProgrammeA roughly 15-month, more advanced programme covering governance, drafting, and specialised laws.
5. Practical TrainingComplete ICSI's required practical/management training under a practising CS or company.
6. MembershipApply to ICSI for membership and officially become a Company Secretary.

ICSI has revised its syllabus and training structure in recent cycles (including a 2022 syllabus update and a further fee/training revision from 2026), so exact durations and requirements can shift — always confirm current details on the official ICSI website before registering.

Know what you're up against

Papers, levels & passing criteria

Every paper is out of 100 marks (CSEET is out of 200 across its four parts). The same passing rule applies at every level.

Papers, levels & passing criteria

LevelPapers
CSEET (4 parts, single sitting)1. Business Communication (50 marks) · 2. Legal Aptitude, Logical Reasoning & Quantitative Aptitude (70 marks) · 3. Economic & Business Environment (50 marks) · 4. Current Affairs (30 marks)
Executive — Module 11. Jurisprudence, Interpretation & General Laws · 2. Company Law & Practice · 3. Setting Up of Business, Industrial & Labour Laws · 4. Corporate Accounting & Financial Management
Executive — Module 25. Capital Market & Securities Laws · 6. Economic, Business & Commercial Laws · 7. Tax Laws & Practice
Professional — Module 11. Environmental, Social & Governance (ESG) – Principles & Practice · 2. Drafting, Pleadings & Appearances · 3. Compliance Management, Audit & Due Diligence
Professional — Module 24. Elective 1 (choose one, e.g. CSR & Social Governance, Intellectual Property Rights, or Artificial Intelligence (AI), Data Analytics & Cyber Security Laws) · 5. Strategic Management & Corporate Finance · 6. Corporate Restructuring, Valuation & Insolvency
Professional — Elective 27. Elective 2 (choose one, e.g. Arbitration, Mediation & Conciliation, or Insolvency & Bankruptcy Law & Practice)

Passing criteria (same rule at every level): you must score at least 40% in each individual paper and at least 50% in aggregate across that level or module, in the same sitting. CSEET has no negative marking; falling short on either the per-paper or the aggregate condition means that level is not cleared.

  • Executive and Professional are each split into modules; you can attempt one module or all of them together, with a "set-off" allowed when taking modules together (surplus marks in one can cover a shortfall in the other's aggregate, as long as you still clear 40% in every paper).
  • Score 60%+ in a paper but still miss your module's overall pass mark, and ICSI grants an exemption in that paper for your next attempt.
  • Professional's electives mean the exact paper you sit for slots 4 and 7 depends on which options you pick.

This reflects ICSI's New Syllabus (2022), the current scheme as of this writing. ICSI periodically revises paper names, elective options and module structure — always cross-check the latest scheme and requirements on icsi.edu before you register.

Official ICSI fees across all levels typically add up to roughly ₹40,000–60,000 — among the lowest of any Indian professional qualification. Most students spend a total of about ₹1–2 lakh once coaching and study material are included. ICSI revised its fee structure in 2026, so check icsi.edu for the current attempt's exact figures. SC/ST, differently-abled and a few other categories are eligible for meaningful fee concessions.

Indicative figures

CS salary in India

Actual pay varies widely by employer, city and specialisation — these are broad, commonly reported ranges, not guarantees.

Fresher₹3–8 LPA

Better training placements (listed companies, law firms) push offers higher.

Mid-career (4–8 yrs)₹10–20 LPA

Faster growth with SEBI, FEMA, IBC or ESG specialisation.

Senior / KMP₹30–60 LPA+

Company Secretary & Compliance Officer roles in large listed companies pay the most, occasionally crossing ₹1 crore.

CS professionals who add a Chartered Accountant (CA), Bachelor of Laws (LLB) or Master of Business Administration (MBA) qualification, or specialise in SEBI filings, FEMA advisory or insolvency resolution, typically command a noticeable premium over generalist compliance roles. ICSI also has mutual recognition arrangements with governance bodies in the UK, Singapore and elsewhere, opening international opportunities for experienced professionals.

Looking ahead

Future scope & the impact of AI

Routine form-filling and document tracking are increasingly handled by compliance-management software, and this will continue. But interpreting new regulations, drafting board resolutions and agreements, advising on governance risk, and taking legal accountability for a company's filings still require a qualified human — and regulatory complexity in India (SEBI, FEMA, IBC, ESG disclosure norms) has generally been increasing, not shrinking.

Growth in startups preparing for listing, and stricter governance norms for mid-sized companies, are both expanding where CS professionals are needed. The most future-proof CS professionals build depth in a specific area — SEBI compliance, M&A, insolvency, or ESG — rather than staying purely generalist.

Honest take

Advantages and disadvantages

Advantages

  • Among the cheapest professional qualifications in India relative to its long-term earning potential.
  • Direct access to the boardroom — a strategic advisory role, not administrative support.
  • Can be pursued alongside a regular degree, and offers both in-house and independent practice routes.

Disadvantages

  • Starting salaries are often lower than CA or engineering until a few years of experience build up.
  • Large, law-heavy syllabus with meaningful attrition at the Executive and Professional levels.
  • Still less well understood by families and students than CA or engineering, despite strong outcomes.
Common myths

Myths vs facts

MYTH A Company Secretary just handles paperwork and minutes. ↓
FACT

A CS is legally accountable for a company's compliance and directly advises the board — closer to legal counsel than administrative support.

MYTH You need an LLB before doing CS. ↓
FACT

CSEET and the CS programme are open to any stream straight after Class 12 — no law degree required.

MYTH CS is always a lower-paying alternative to CA. ↓
FACT

Starting pay is often lower, but senior CS professionals in listed companies can earn salaries on par with, or exceeding, many CA roles.
