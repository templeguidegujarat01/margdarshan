9 min read · UGC-recognised undergraduate degree · Open after Class 12, Commerce preferred

When Indian students search "Bachelor of Accounting," what they usually find is Bachelor of Accounting and Finance (BAF) — Bachelor of Accounting and Finance, a specialised 3-year degree offered at colleges like SRCC and Hindu College in Delhi, across University of Mumbai-affiliated colleges, at Gujarat University, and at many others nationally. It goes deeper and earlier into accounting, cost accounting, taxation and auditing than a general B.Com — making it one of the most direct routes into a Chartered Accountant (CA), Certified Public Accountant (CPA) or Association of Chartered Certified Accountants (ACCA) career. Overview

What is Bachelor of Accounting (BAF)?

BAF is a 3-year specialised undergraduate degree covering financial accounting, cost accounting, corporate accounting, taxation (GST and Income Tax), auditing and business law. It is offered at reputed Delhi University colleges including SRCC and Hindu College, across University of Mumbai-affiliated colleges, at Gujarat University, and at numerous other universities across India — making it considerably more widely available than Bachelor of Financial Markets (BFM) (Bachelor of Financial Markets).

Where a general B.Com introduces accounting alongside a broad mix of business, economics and law subjects, BAF front-loads accounting, cost accounting and taxation more intensively from the first year — closer in spirit to a direct CA-preparation track than to a general commerce degree.

Fit check

Who should choose BAF

✔ Ideal for you if…

  • Already know you're aiming for CA, CPA or ACCA and want a degree that builds directly toward it.
  • Genuinely enjoy detailed, rule-based work — accounting standards, tax provisions, audit procedures.
  • Want a degree that's genuinely available nationwide, not concentrated in one city or state.

✖ Reconsider if…

  • Are drawn to stock markets, trading or investment banking specifically — BFM is the closer match.
  • Are still undecided between accounting, management and general business — B.Com keeps more doors open at once.
  • Dislike repetitive, detail-heavy compliance work — accounting and auditing involve a lot of precision and procedure.
Eligibility

Who can apply for BAF

Wide range by institution

Fees & scholarships

BAF fees vary considerably depending on the city and college type. Government and government-aided colleges, including many Mumbai University-affiliated ones, can be very affordable — some report a total 3-year tuition fee as low as roughly ₹54,000. Reputed private colleges such as St. Xavier's Mumbai charge substantially more, with total fees in the range of ₹2-2.7 lakh for the full course. Across India as a whole, BAF fees are commonly reported anywhere from roughly ₹30,000 to ₹6.5 lakh for the full programme, depending heavily on the institution. Merit-based and SC/ST scholarships are available at most government colleges. Always confirm current fees directly on your target college's official admission page.

Beyond the degree

Higher studies

BAF's accounting-heavy syllabus makes CA preparation noticeably smoother, since several core concepts overlap directly with the Institute of Chartered Accountants of India (ICAI) curriculum. It's also a strong base for international qualifications like CPA (US) or ACCA (UK), and for further study through M.Com, a Master of Business Administration (MBA) in Finance, or certifications like Chartered Financial Analyst (CFA) or Financial Risk Manager (FRM) for students who want to move toward financial analysis rather than pure accounting.

Honest take

Advantages and disadvantages

Advantages

  • One of the most direct undergraduate routes into CA, CPA and ACCA preparation.
  • Genuinely available nationwide — DU, Mumbai University, Gujarat University and many more — unlike Maharashtra-concentrated BFM.
  • Stable, evergreen demand — every company needs accounting, audit and tax compliance regardless of economic cycles.

Disadvantages

  • Narrower than B.Com — less useful if you later want to pivot into general management or markets-focused finance.
  • A lot of detail-heavy, rule-based compliance work, which doesn't suit everyone.
  • On its own, doesn't replace CA/CPA/ACCA — the highest-paying accounting roles still need one of these added on.
