What is CFA?

CFA stands for Chartered Financial Analyst. It is one of the world's most respected qualifications in investment management and finance.

The CFA Program is administered by the CFA Institute and focuses on investment analysis, portfolio management, financial markets, ethics and wealth management.

CFA professionals work in investment banking, equity research, portfolio management, wealth management, asset management and financial consulting.

Eligibility for CFA

The CFA Program is designed for students and professionals who want to build careers in investment management and finance.

  • Students can begin planning for CFA during graduation.
  • B.Com, BBA, BCA, MBA, CA, CS and CMA students commonly pursue CFA.
  • Finance, Economics and Business students often benefit the most.
  • Strong interest in investments and financial markets is helpful.

CFA Program Structure

The CFA qualification consists of three levels that progressively build advanced finance and investment knowledge.

  • Level I: Investment tools, ethics and financial concepts.
  • Level II: Asset valuation and financial analysis.
  • Level III: Portfolio management and wealth planning.

Candidates must successfully complete all levels to progress toward becoming a CFA Charterholder.

Career Opportunities After CFA

CFA is one of the strongest qualifications for careers in finance, investments and capital markets.

Many CFA professionals work in investment firms, banks, asset management companies, hedge funds, consulting firms and multinational corporations.

Investment Banking Career

Investment Banking is one of the most prestigious and competitive finance careers.

Investment bankers help companies raise capital, issue securities, manage mergers and acquisitions, and execute large financial transactions.

CFA knowledge is highly respected in investment banking because it provides strong expertise in valuation, financial analysis and corporate finance.

Equity Research Career

Equity Research Analysts study publicly listed companies and evaluate whether their shares are attractive investments.

  • Analyze company financial statements.
  • Evaluate industry trends.
  • Forecast future earnings.
  • Prepare investment reports.
  • Recommend Buy, Hold or Sell decisions.

Portfolio Management Career

Portfolio Managers manage investments on behalf of individuals, institutions and investment funds.

Their objective is to maximize returns while controlling risk according to investment goals.

  • Stock Selection
  • Bond Investments
  • Asset Allocation
  • Risk Management
  • Performance Monitoring

Asset Management Career

Asset Management professionals oversee large pools of investments for clients and institutions.

They make strategic investment decisions to generate long-term wealth and financial growth.

CFA vs CA

CFA:
Focuses on Investments, Portfolio Management, Financial Markets, Equity Research and Wealth Management.


CA:
Focuses on Accounting, Auditing, Taxation, Financial Reporting and Compliance.


Both are highly respected qualifications but serve different career paths.

CFA vs MBA Finance

CFA:
Specialized finance and investment qualification with deep focus on investment management.


MBA Finance:
Broader management degree covering finance along with leadership, marketing and business management.


Many professionals pursue both CFA and MBA to strengthen their career profile.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)
