BJMC — Journalism & Mass Communication

9 min read · UGC-recognised undergraduate degree · Open after Class 12, any stream

Bachelor of Journalism and Mass Communication (BJMC) trains students to report, write, produce and communicate across print, television, radio and digital platforms — covering news writing, media production, public relations and advertising. It's a practical, industry-facing Arts degree, but success depends heavily on building a real portfolio and network alongside the coursework. This guide covers exactly what the degree involves and where it leads.

Overview

What is BJMC?

  • BJMC is a 3-year undergraduate degree (4 years for Honours with Research under National Education Policy (NEP) 2020) that trains students in news reporting and writing, media production for TV and radio, digital and social media, public relations, and advertising.
  • Coursework blends theory — media law and ethics, communication theory — with hands-on practice: writing news stories, producing short videos and podcasts, and building a portfolio of published or produced work.
  • It's offered at institutions like the Indian Institute of Mass Communication (IIMC), Delhi University's affiliated colleges, Symbiosis Institute of Media and Communication, and Xavier Institute of Communications, among many others, under the University Grants Commission (UGC) framework.
Fit check

Who should choose BJMC

✔ Ideal for you if…

  • Enjoy writing, storytelling, and following current events with genuine curiosity.
  • Are comfortable with a practical, deadline-driven degree involving real reporting and production work, not just classroom theory.
  • Are willing to actively build a portfolio — internships, published clips, a demo reel — alongside the degree.

✖ Reconsider if…

  • Dislike tight deadlines and public-facing work — journalism and media production are fast-paced by nature.
  • Expect the degree alone to guarantee a job — portfolio, internships and networking matter as much as the coursework.
  • Are choosing it purely because it seems "glamorous" — entry-level media work is often demanding for modest early pay.
Eligibility

Who can apply for BJMC

  • ✓Class 12 pass (10+2) from a recognised board, typically 45-50% aggregate, varying by institute.
  • ✓Open to students from any stream — Arts, Commerce or Science — since no specific Class 12 subject is compulsory.
  • ✓Strong written and spoken English (or the relevant regional language) genuinely matters, since it's assessed directly in most entrance processes.

Wide range by institute

Fees & scholarships

BJMC fees vary considerably by institute. Government and central-university colleges are relatively affordable, typically ₹20,000-80,000 per year, while reputed private media schools can charge ₹1-3 lakh per year given the practical equipment and production facilities involved. Merit-based scholarships are available at several institutes. Always confirm current fees directly on your target college's official admission page.

Beyond the degree

Higher studies

A Master of Arts (MA) in Journalism/Mass Communication — at institutions like IIMC, Jamia Millia Islamia's AJK Mass Communication Research Centre, or Symbiosis — is a natural next step for students wanting deeper specialisation or academic/research roles. Others pursue a Master of Business Administration (MBA) in Marketing or specialised certifications in digital marketing, data journalism or video editing to strengthen a specific career direction.

Honest take

Advantages and disadvantages

Advantages

  • Practical, industry-facing degree with real reporting and production experience built in.
  • Genuinely diverse outcomes — journalism, PR, advertising, digital media and corporate communications.
  • Growing demand from digital and social media, alongside traditional print and broadcast media.

Disadvantages

  • Entry-level media salaries, especially in regional outlets, can be modest relative to the demanding, deadline-driven workload.
  • Portfolio and network matter as much as the degree — passive students without internships struggle more at placement time.
  • The media industry is undergoing rapid change, requiring continuous adaptation to new digital formats and platforms.

Common myths

Myths vs facts

MYTH BJMC only leads to becoming a TV news anchor. ↓
FACT

Graduates work across print, digital journalism, PR, advertising, corporate communications and content strategy — TV anchoring is one visible, competitive path among many.

MYTH You need to be extroverted or "camera-ready" to succeed in this field. ↓
FACT

Many BJMC careers — writing, editing, digital strategy, research — are behind-the-scenes roles that reward strong writing and analytical skills more than on-camera presence.

MYTH The degree alone guarantees a media job. ↓
FACT

Internships, a genuine portfolio of published or produced work, and networking within the industry matter just as much as classroom learning for landing a strong first role.
