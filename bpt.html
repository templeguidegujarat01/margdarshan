BPT — Bachelor of Physiotherapy

10 min read · University-recognised allied-health degree · Open after Class 12 with PCB — NEET generally not required

BPT stands apart from every other medical degree covered so far in one key way: it doesn't require NEET, admitting instead through state-level entrance tests or direct Class 12 PCB merit. It's also a field whose national regulation is still maturing — unlike Nursing's long-established council, physiotherapy is only now getting a unified national framework. What it does have is one of the strongest, best-documented international licensing pathways of any Indian allied-health degree.

Overview

What is BPT?

BPT (Bachelor of Physiotherapy) is a professional degree focused on physical rehabilitation — helping patients recover movement and function after injuries, surgeries, paralysis, sports injuries and physical disabilities, using exercise therapy, manual therapy and electrotherapy.

India's demographic trends genuinely favour this field: an ageing population, rising chronic disease, and growing awareness of sports injury management and rehabilitation are all real, ongoing drivers of demand. What's genuinely worth understanding honestly before enrolling: physiotherapy in India has historically lacked the kind of single, long-established national regulatory council that Nursing (Indian Nursing Council) or Pharmacy (Pharmacy Council of India) have had for decades. The NCAHP Act 2021 is progressively closing this gap, with a national physiotherapy register expected to be operational by 2026-27.

Fit check

Who should choose BPT

BPT is a good fit if you...

  • Genuinely enjoy hands-on, physical patient interaction and helping people regain mobility and function.
  • Are interested in the genuinely strong international licensing pathway as a long-term career option.
  • Are comfortable actively researching and staying current on your specific state's evolving registration requirements.

Think twice if you...

  • Expect the same national regulatory maturity that MBBS or Nursing already have — BPT's framework is genuinely still catching up.
  • Want a guaranteed high starting salary in India — fresher pay is genuinely modest.
  • Dislike physically demanding work — physiotherapy involves real physical exertion assisting patients.
Eligibility

Who can apply for BPT

  • Class 12 pass with Physics, Chemistry and Biology (PCB) — Biology is compulsory.
  • NEET is generally not required — most states use their own CET or direct Class 12 PCB merit, unlike MBBS, BDS, BAMS or BHMS.
  • Always verify your specific state and target college's exact requirement, since a small number of programmes vary.

Genuinely affordable at government colleges

Fees & scholarships

Government BPT colleges are reported to charge as little as ₹20,000 per year, meaning a full 4.5-year total in the range of roughly ₹1-1.5 lakh — genuinely one of the more affordable medical-adjacent degrees available. Private colleges start from around ₹1.2 lakh per year and go considerably higher, with total 4.5-year costs commonly in the ₹6-15 lakh range depending on the institute. Always confirm current fees directly on your target college's official admission page.

Specialisation and international licensing

Higher studies

MPT (Master of Physiotherapy) is a 2-year postgraduate specialisation — Orthopaedics, Neurology, Sports and Paediatric physiotherapy among the common tracks — reported to open higher-paying specialist and academic roles. For students aiming abroad, UK (HCPC registration), US (NPTE plus state licensure), Australia (AHPRA registration), Canada, and Gulf countries (DHA/MOH/HAAD registration) all offer established, well-documented licensing pathways, with those markets facing reported ongoing physiotherapist shortages.

Honest take

Advantages and disadvantages

Advantages

  • Genuinely growing field driven by real demographic trends — ageing population, chronic disease, sports injury awareness.
  • Comparatively accessible admission via state CET/merit, without needing NEET.
  • Among the strongest, most well-documented international licensing pathways of any Indian allied-health degree.

Disadvantages

  • National regulatory framework is still maturing — unlike Nursing (INC), physiotherapy lacks a single long-established national council, though NCAHP Act 2021 implementation is closing this gap.
  • Fresher salaries in India are genuinely modest relative to the physical and educational demands of the degree.
  • Involves genuine physical exertion assisting patients, which isn't sustainable for everyone long-term without good practice management.

Common myths

Myths vs facts

Myth: You need NEET to become a physiotherapist.

Fact: Unlike MBBS, BDS, BAMS and BHMS, BPT admission is generally through state-level CET or direct Class 12 PCB merit — NEET is not required at most colleges, though a few programmes vary by state, so always verify locally.

Myth: Physiotherapy has the same strong national regulatory framework as nursing or pharmacy.

Fact: Physiotherapy in India has historically lacked a single unified national council the way Nursing (INC) or Pharmacy (PCI) have; the NCAHP Act 2021 is progressively building this framework, with a national physiotherapy register expected operational by 2026-27 — genuinely worth understanding as you plan your registration and practice.

Myth: Physiotherapy salaries in India are uniformly low with no real upside.

Fact: While Indian fresher salaries are genuinely modest, BPT has one of the strongest, most well-documented international licensing pathways among Indian allied-health degrees, with the UK, US, Australia, Canada and Gulf countries all facing physiotherapist shortages and offering meaningfully higher pay after registration.
