Overview

What is Genetics?

Genetics is the branch of biology that studies genes, DNA, heredity, and how traits pass from parents to offspring. It helps scientists understand life at the molecular level and develop solutions for inherited diseases, agriculture and biotechnology — genomics, genetic engineering, molecular genetics and population genetics are all major areas within the field.

Genomic testing, precision medicine and biotechnology are all genuinely expanding, with industry outlooks citing over 20% projected job growth reflecting this demand. Here's the honest, important context though: Genetics as a standalone career is even more research and PhD-dependent than the closely related Biotechnology field. Most substantial roles — genomics research, gene therapy, academic positions — require advanced study, with one clear, genuine exception worth knowing about early: Genetic Counseling.

Fit check

Who should choose Genetics

Genetics is a good fit if you...

  • Are genuinely curious about DNA, heredity and how genetic information shapes health and disease.
  • Are prepared to pursue an MSc or PhD for the field's most substantial research roles.
  • Are interested specifically in Genetic Counseling as a genuine, patient-facing clinical alternative to pure research.

Think twice if you...

  • Want to avoid postgraduate study — Genetics rewards further study even more than Biotechnology does.
  • Are drawn in by inflated online salary claims — this field's salary content is genuinely inconsistent online.
  • Want a broader applied-industry degree with more built-in non-PhD paths — Biotechnology is the closer match.
Eligibility

Who can apply for Genetics

  • Class 12 pass with Physics, Chemistry and Biology (PCB), or PCMB — Mathematics is helpful in some programmes.
  • NEET is not required — admission is via CUET, university-specific tests, state-level admissions, or specialised research-oriented exams like the IISER Aptitude Test or NEST.
  • Many universities offer interdisciplinary programmes combining Genetics with Biotechnology, Bioinformatics and Molecular Biology.

Comparatively affordable, research-focused route

Fees & scholarships

Undergraduate Genetics programmes commonly cost ₹3-4 lakh for the full course at many institutes, with government and university-affiliated colleges toward the lower end. Research-track students should specifically look into DBT (Department of Biotechnology) fellowships and grants, which support many MSc and PhD students financially throughout their research training. Always confirm current fees and available scholarships directly on your target institute's official admission page.

The field's central honest truth

Higher studies

An MSc in Genetics or Genomics is genuinely close to a baseline expectation for meaningful research roles, and a PhD is typically required for academic positions, gene therapy research, and leadership roles in genomics companies. The clearest non-PhD alternate path is a Master's in Genetic Counseling — a growing, patient-facing clinical specialisation that doesn't require a doctorate, and is reported to offer real earning growth (₹2-3 LPA entry to ₹12 LPA experienced) as India's genomic testing sector expands.

Common myths

Myths vs facts

Myth: Genetics graduates earn ₹17-28 LPA on average, as some sources claim.

Fact: This field's online salary content is unusually inconsistent. Credible, cross-checked figures for freshers cluster around ₹3-4 LPA for research/lab roles, with Genetic Counselors starting around ₹2-3 LPA — treat very high "average" claims with real skepticism.

Myth: Genetics and Biotechnology offer the same career flexibility.

Fact: Genetics is genuinely more research and PhD-dependent as a standalone career than Biotechnology, which has more built-in non-PhD paths like Regulatory Affairs. Genetics' clearest non-PhD alternative is specifically Genetic Counseling.

Myth: You need a PhD for every genetics-related job.

Fact: Genetic Counseling generally requires a relevant Master's degree, not a PhD, and offers real patient-facing clinical work as India's genomic testing sector expands — a genuine exception to the field's research-heavy norm.
