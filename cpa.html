US CPA — Certified Public Accountant

12 min read · Regulated by AICPA & NASBA (USA) · Needs 120 credit hours to sit the exam

US Certified Public Accountant (CPA) is the American equivalent of Chartered Accountant (CA) — a licence issued by individual US state boards, developed by AICPA and administered with NASBA. It's one of the fastest professional qualifications to complete (often 12-18 months of exams) and is heavily recruited by Big 4 US-practice teams and Global Capability Centres handling US GAAP and SEC-reporting work out of India.

Overview

What is a US CPA?

  • CPA is a license issued by individual US state boards of accountancy, with the Uniform CPA Examination itself developed by the American Institute of Certified Public Accountants (AICPA) and coordinated with the National Association of State Boards of Accountancy (NASBA).
  • It's the US equivalent of India's CA — the credential that authorises accounting practice under US rules.
  • For Indian professionals, CPA isn't usually a replacement for CA — it doesn't grant Indian statutory audit rights.
Read moreRead less↓
  • It's most valuable as a specialised addition: proof of deep US GAAP and SEC-reporting expertise, which is exactly what Big 4 US-practice teams and India-based Global Capability Centres of American multinationals are hiring for at scale.
Fit check

Who should choose CPA — and who should think twice

✔ Ideal for you if…

  • Are already a qualified CA, Cost and Management Accountant (CMA) or postgraduate and want a fast, focused addition (just 4 exam sections).
  • Want to work with US GAAP, SEC filings, or US-headquartered MNC finance teams.
  • Want the shortest realistic timeline among major accounting qualifications (often 12-18 months of exams).

✖ Reconsider if…

  • Only have a standard 3-year B.Com — you'll likely need an extra qualification first to reach 120 credits.
  • Want Indian statutory audit rights specifically — that's CA territory, not CPA.
  • Are cost-sensitive — India's international testing surcharge makes CPA one of the pricier options per exam attempt.
A credit-hour system, not a stream requirement

Eligibility — the 120/150 credit rule

US state boards use a US credit-hour system, not Indian degree titles directly. Your Indian qualifications are converted into equivalent US credits by an approved evaluation agency (NIES, WES or FACS).

Eligibility — the 120/150 credit rule

Your qualificationApprox. US credit value
B.Com / BBA / BA (Economics) / BMS (3-year degree)~90 credits
CA Inter / CMA Inter / a 1-year postgraduate diploma+ ~30 credits
M.Com / MBA / qualified CA / CS / CMA+ ~60 credits

To sit the exam: you need 120 credits — most B.Com-only graduates need to add CA Inter, CMA Inter, or a postgraduate diploma to get there. To get licensed (not just pass the exam): you need 150 credits, which usually means a full postgraduate degree or a completed professional qualification like CA, CS or CMA. Exact credit conversions and accounting-specific credit minimums vary by state board and evaluation agency — always get your own transcripts formally evaluated before assuming eligibility.

3 Core + 1 Discipline — every section explained

SectionFull nameCovers

FAR (Core)Financial Accounting and ReportingUS GAAP financial statements — traditionally the most content-heavy section
REG (Core)RegulationUS federal taxation, business law, ethics and professional responsibilities
BAR (Discipline)Business Analysis and ReportingAdvanced financial reporting, business performance analysis — best paired with FAR strength

Every candidate takes all 3 Core sections; you choose exactly 1 of the 3 Discipline sections based on your career direction. This replaced the old fixed 4-section exam (which included a general "BEC" section) starting January 2024.

A single scaled score per section

Passing criteria

Each section is scored on a scale of 0-99, and you need a minimum scaled score of 75 to pass — there's no separate "group" or aggregate concept like ICAI/ICSI/ICMAI use. Once you pass your first section, you generally have an 18-month rolling window to pass the remaining three (this window has been extended and standardised across jurisdictions in recent years) — plan your section order and timing around it.

Common mistake: saving the Discipline section for last without checking the quarterly window dates. Since Discipline sections only run in specific one-month windows, poor timing here can add months to your overall timeline even if you're otherwise ready.
Beyond the exams

Ethics exam & work experience

Passing all 4 sections doesn't complete your licence on its own. Most state boards additionally require:

  • An open-book Ethics exam, separate from the main CPA exam.
  • 1-2 years of relevant accounting experience (commonly cited as roughly 2,000 hours), verified and signed off by a licensed CPA supervisor.

Requirements vary meaningfully by state board — some are more accommodating for international candidates than others, which is why choosing the right state board early is a genuine strategic decision, not a formality.

India's international testing surcharge is among the higher rates NASBA charges globally, so testing costs here run noticeably above what a US-based candidate pays for the identical exam. All fees are billed in USD, so your INR outlay moves with the exchange rate — always verify current published fees on nasba.org and your chosen state board's website before paying.

Set expectations correctly

Difficulty & realistic duration

Exam difficulty is generally considered high but manageable with structured preparation — FAR is consistently reported as the most content-heavy Core section, while ISC is often seen as the most approachable Discipline choice. Most well-prepared candidates cite roughly 300-500 hours of study per section.

The exams themselves are typically completed in 12-18 months once you're eligible to sit them — but the total journey, including any extra degree needed to reach 120 credits, the 150-credit licensing requirement, and 1-2 years of work experience, is realistically a multi-year path for someone starting from a standard B.Com alone.

Looking ahead

Future scope & the impact of AI

Much of the routine reconciliation and reporting work in GCC finance teams is being reshaped by automation and AI-driven tools — a trend CPA Evolution itself was partly designed around, by adding the ISC Discipline focused on information systems and controls. But interpreting US GAAP judgment calls, managing SEC-reporting risk, and advising on complex tax positions remain squarely human work, and India's GCC sector — the biggest employer of India-based CPAs — has been expanding rapidly, not shrinking.

CPAs who build data-systems literacy (the ISC discipline, or general data analytics skills) alongside their core technical training tend to be best positioned for where the profession is heading.

Honest take

Advantages and disadvantages

Advantages

  • One of the fastest professional qualifications to complete — often 12-18 months of exams for eligible candidates.
  • Just 4 exam sections, with only 1 chosen based on your specialisation.
  • Strong salary premium in India's rapidly growing GCC and Big 4 US-practice markets.

Disadvantages

  • Credit-hour eligibility is genuinely confusing, and a plain B.Com often isn't enough on its own.
  • India's international testing surcharge makes it one of the costlier options per exam attempt.
  • No Indian statutory audit rights — it's a complement to CA for most Indian careers, not a substitute.
Common myths

Myths vs facts

MYTH Any B.Com graduate can walk in and sit the CPA exam. ↓
FACT

A standard B.Com typically evaluates to only ~90 US credits — most graduates need an additional qualification to reach the 120 required to sit the exam.

MYTH CPA lets you sign statutory audits in India, just like a CA. ↓
FACT

Indian statutory audit signing rights remain reserved for ICAI-qualified CAs — CPA is valued for US GAAP and SEC-reporting work instead.

MYTH CPA and CA are essentially interchangeable qualifications. ↓
FACT

They're built for different regulatory systems — CPA for US accounting rules, CA for Indian ones. Many professionals hold both because they serve genuinely different purposes.

Practical advice

Expert tips for CPA candidates

  • Get your credential evaluation done early — it's the single biggest bottleneck before you can even apply to a state board.
  • Choose your state board deliberately — some are meaningfully more accommodating to international candidates than others, and application fees vary.
  • Pick your Discipline section based on your career direction, then start the matching Core section first (FAR before BAR, AUD before ISC, REG before TCP) to reuse what you've just studied.
  • Plan your Discipline section around its quarterly window well in advance — missing it can cost months.
