Performing Arts — Dance, Music & Theatre

It's genuinely rewarding creative work, but income can be irregular, and most performers build stability by combining performance with teaching or choreography. Overview

What is a career in Performing Arts?

  • Performing Arts spans classical dance forms (Bharatanatyam, Kathak, Odissi and others), Indian classical and contemporary music, and theatre — all built on sustained, disciplined training and live performance skill.
  • Unlike most careers on this site, formal training here often starts outside the university system entirely — through a guru-shishya (teacher-disciple) tradition for classical dance and music, or conservatory-style institutes, with formal degrees available as a later or parallel option.
  • For theatre specifically, the National School of Drama (NSD) in Delhi is India's most prestigious training ground, alongside university theatre departments and repertory companies.

  • Music training ranges from classical gharanas and conservatories to contemporary music schools teaching Western instruments and production.
Fit check

Who should choose Performing Arts

✔ Ideal for you if…

  • Already have, or are willing to build, a sustained, disciplined practice in your chosen discipline.
  • Are comfortable with public performance and ongoing artistic critique.
  • Are genuinely open to combining performance with teaching or choreography for income stability.

✖ Reconsider if…

  • Need predictable, fixed income immediately — performance income especially can be genuinely irregular.
  • Are starting from zero and expecting rapid mastery — classical dance and music genuinely take years of sustained practice.
  • Dislike physical rigour and repetition — technical mastery in dance and music comes through extensive repetitive practice.
Training routes differ by discipline

How training actually works

  • ✓Classical dance/music: Typically trained through a guru or established institution over many years, often starting in childhood, though serious training can begin later too.
  • ✓Theatre: A 3-year diploma at NSD (highly competitive, national entrance), or university theatre/drama departments and repertory companies.
  • ✓Contemporary music: Music schools teaching Western instruments, vocals and production, alongside self-taught and online-learning routes for many contemporary genres.

Honest take

Advantages and disadvantages

Advantages

  • Deeply fulfilling, expressive work with a rich cultural tradition to draw from in India.
  • Multiple realistic income streams — teaching, choreography, performance — beyond just live shows.
  • Potential to move into film, TV or established touring careers for the most successful performers.

Disadvantages

  • Performance income is genuinely irregular for most performers, especially early on.
  • Mastery takes years of sustained, often physically demanding practice.
  • Highly competitive at the top — NSD and leading conservatories admit only a small number of applicants each year.
Common myths

Myths vs facts

MYTH You can only succeed in performing arts if you started training as a young child. ↓
FACT

Starting young helps, especially for classical forms, but many performers, particularly in theatre and contemporary dance, build genuine careers after starting serious training as teenagers or in college.

MYTH Performing arts careers always mean unstable, poverty-level income. ↓
FACT

Many performers build stable income by combining performance with teaching, choreography or arts administration — full instability isn't the norm for disciplined, business-savvy performers.

MYTH You need a formal degree to be taken seriously as a performer. ↓
FACT

In classical dance and music especially, guru-shishya training and demonstrated performance skill often carry more weight than a formal degree — though institutes like NSD do offer significant added credibility for theatre.
