Overview

What is RBI Grade B?

The RBI recruits Officers in Grade B (DR) – General, and also DEPR (Economic & Policy Research) and DSIM (Statistics) streams. This page covers the General stream, which is open to graduates of any discipline meeting the percentage criteria.

Grade B officers work in departments such as banking regulation, supervision, foreign exchange, currency management and financial inclusion, in Mumbai and regional offices.

Fit check

Who should choose RBI Grade B

A good fit if you...

  • Are interested in economics, finance and policy.
  • Can write analytical descriptive answers.
  • Want a high-status, well-paid public sector career.

Think twice if you...

  • Do not meet the 60% graduation criterion (or 55% Postgraduate (PG)).
  • Dislike reading economic and financial news.
  • Want a customer-facing sales role.

What gives you an edge

Economics & Commerce

Directly useful for Economic & Social Issues and Finance & Management papers.

Any discipline

Eligible if percentage criteria are met — engineers and science graduates also clear it.

Writing skills

Phase II has descriptive answers and an English writing paper.

Step-by-step plan for your background

Arts / Humanities

If you're from Arts

  1. Economics or Political Science graduates have a head start in Economic & Social Issues.
  2. Build finance and management concepts from standard books.
  3. Practise typed descriptive answers within time limits.
Commerce

If you're from Commerce

  1. Finance & Management maps directly to your degree.
  2. Strengthen economic and social issues through government reports and the Economic Survey.
  3. Practise reasoning and DI for Phase I.
Science

If you're from Science

  1. Phase I aptitude suits you — but Phase II is descriptive economics and finance.
  2. Start Economic & Social Issues and Finance early; they need the most time.
  3. Read RBI reports and the Union Budget summary.

Preparation strategy

How to prepare for RBI Grade B

A practical, phase-by-phase plan. Adjust the timeline to when the next notification is expected.

1Weeks 1–2

Baseline

  1. Read the notification and syllabus.
  2. Attempt one Prelims mock to measure your level.
2Months 1–2

Prelims foundation

  1. Daily: 20 puzzles/arrangements, 30 quant questions, 1 reading comprehension.
  2. Learn shortcuts for simplification and number series.
3Months 2–3

Mains depth

  1. High-level puzzles, caselet DI and data sufficiency.
  2. Banking, economy and financial awareness notes.
  3. Weekly essay and letter writing.
4Final 6 weeks

Mocks and interview

  1. Prelims and Mains mocks on alternate days with analysis.
  2. For PO-level exams, prepare your introduction, banking basics and current issues for the interview.
