Overview

What is MBBS?

MBBS (Bachelor of Medicine and Bachelor of Surgery) is the primary medical degree required to become a doctor in India. It covers human anatomy, physiology, pathology, pharmacology, and the diagnosis and treatment of disease, combining classroom study with hands-on clinical training in hospitals.

It remains one of India's most respected and stable career paths — but the on-the-ground reality in 2026 is more complicated than most coaching advertisements suggest. Seats are extremely limited relative to demand, fees vary by more than ten times between government and private institutions for an identical degree, and most graduates now pursue further specialisation (NEET-PG) almost by default rather than by choice.

Fit check

Who should choose MBBS

MBBS is a good fit if you...

  • Have a genuine, sustained interest in biology, medicine and patient care — not just family expectation.
  • Are prepared for a genuinely long path — 5.5 years of MBBS, commonly followed by 3+ more years for a specialisation.
  • Have realistically assessed your family's ability to fund a private-college path if a government seat doesn't come through.

Think twice if you...

  • Are choosing MBBS mainly for prestige or family pressure, without genuine interest in the day-to-day work of medicine.
  • Aren't prepared for the real financial commitment a private or deemed-university seat requires without a government seat.
  • Expect quick financial returns — meaningful earning typically starts several years after graduation, once specialised.
Eligibility

Who can apply for MBBS

  • Class 12 pass with Physics, Chemistry and Biology (PCB) from a recognised board, generally with a minimum of 50% aggregate for general category (lower for reserved categories).
  • A qualifying score in NEET-UG is mandatory — there is no route into any recognised MBBS programme in India without it.
  • Government colleges require far higher NEET percentiles (typically 90-99.9+) than private colleges, which accept from the NMC's 50th-percentile minimum upward.

The single biggest financial decision in this career

Fees & scholarships

This is where MBBS differs most from almost every other career on this site. A realistic 5-year all-in budget at a government college, including hostel and living costs, commonly lands around ₹13-15 lakh — genuinely affordable, and AIIMS Delhi specifically charges as little as ₹6,280-7,000 in total tuition for the full course. The same degree at a private college on a state-quota seat commonly costs ₹53-73 lakh, and at a deemed university or full management-quota seat, realistic budgets run ₹1.2-1.47 crore or more. Merit-based fee concessions and state government scholarships exist at many government colleges; private and deemed institutions rarely offer comparable relief. This fee gap, not academic difficulty, is the real deciding factor for most families choosing between government and private admission.

Almost the default next step

Higher studies

NEET-PG, leading to an MD (Doctor of Medicine) or MS (Master of Surgery), is not legally mandatory but has become close to a default next step for most MBBS graduates, since specialisation substantially raises both earning potential and the range of available roles. This typically adds another 3-5 years of preparation and study. Super-specialisation (DM/MCh) follows for those pursuing narrower fields like cardiology or neurosurgery, and some graduates pursue an MBA in Healthcare Management or move into medical research and public health policy instead of clinical practice.

Honest take

Advantages and disadvantages

Advantages

  • One of India's most respected, stable career paths, with healthcare demand that will always exist.
  • Genuinely deep, meaningful work with real, direct impact on people's lives.
  • Extremely affordable at government colleges — a genuine, life-changing opportunity for students who clear the cutoff.

Disadvantages

  • Extremely competitive government-seat admission — roughly 200 aspirants per seat in 2026.
  • Private and deemed-university fees can exceed ₹1 crore for the same degree a government seat costs ₹13-15 lakh for.
  • A genuinely long path — 5.5 years of MBBS, commonly followed by 3+ more years for NEET-PG specialisation before real earning growth.

Common myths

Myths vs facts

Myth: A private MBBS degree is a "lesser" degree than a government one.

Fact: The MBBS curriculum and licensing are standardised nationally by the NMC; private and government graduates are commonly reported to earn similarly after 5-7 years of practice. The real difference is upfront cost, not the value of the qualification itself.

Myth: MBBS alone is enough to become a well-paid specialist doctor.

Fact: MBBS makes you a general physician; becoming a specialist (cardiologist, surgeon, and similar) requires clearing NEET-PG and completing an MD or MS, adding several more years of study.

Myth: Doctors start earning very high salaries immediately after MBBS.

Fact: The mandatory internship year pays a modest stipend (₹15,000-35,000/month), and even a fresh MBBS graduate without further specialisation typically earns ₹6-12 LPA — meaningful earning growth generally comes several years later, with specialisation and experience.
