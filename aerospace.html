Overview

What is Aerospace Engineering?

  • Aerospace Engineering is a 4-year engineering degree focused on the design, development, testing and maintenance of aircraft, spacecraft, satellites, missiles and drones.
  • Its foundation rests on aerodynamics (how air flows over a body), propulsion (jet and rocket engines), aircraft structures, flight mechanics and avionics.
  • For most of its history in India, this field meant one thing: government.
Read more
  • That's genuinely changing — India's updated space policy has opened the door to private space startups like Skyroot Aerospace and Agnikul Cosmos, alongside expanding commercial aviation and MRO (Maintenance, Repair, Overhaul) hubs from Boeing and Airbus.
  • It's still a smaller job market than core branches like Mechanical or Civil, but it's no longer only ISRO or nothing.
Fit check

Who should choose Aerospace Engineering

✔ Ideal for you if…

  • Have a genuine, specific interest in flight or space — not just excitement about the idea of it.
  • Are willing to prepare seriously for GATE or a government exam like ICRB/SET, since that's the route to the field's best-paying roles.
  • Are open to relocating — most aerospace jobs are concentrated in Bengaluru, Hyderabad and Thiruvananthapuram.

✖ Reconsider if…

  • Assume ISRO is guaranteed after graduation — recruitment is highly competitive with limited seats.
  • Want the wide private-sector job market of a branch like CSE or Mechanical — aerospace-specific private roles in India remain genuinely limited.
  • Aren't willing to put in extra years of GATE/government-exam preparation on top of the degree itself.
Eligibility

Who can apply for Aerospace Engineering

  • ✓Class 12 pass with Physics, Chemistry and Mathematics (PCM) from a recognised board — this field is exceptionally Maths and Physics-heavy.
  • ✓For Indian Institutes of Technology (IITs) via Joint Entrance Examination (JEE), a minimum of 75% aggregate in Class 12 (65% for SC/ST) is required for counselling eligibility.
  • ✓IIST Thiruvananthapuram admits through JEE Advanced rank under a separate counselling process, with a scholarship-cum-service bond tied to future work at ISRO.

The primary national entrance exam for B.Tech admission at NITs, IIITs and most state/private engineering colleges.

JEE Advanced

The follow-on exam (only for qualified JEE Main candidates) required specifically for admission to the IITs.

Wide range by institute type

Fees & scholarships

Government-funded institutes remain the most economical route: total fees for the full 4-year Aerospace Engineering programme at IITs commonly fall in the ₹2-10 lakh range, with fee waivers available based on family income and category. IIST Thiruvananthapuram is a distinctive case — its scholarship-cum-service bond scheme covers most fees in exchange for a service commitment to ISRO after graduation. Private colleges are considerably more expensive — total costs of roughly ₹8-20 lakh are typical. Always confirm current fees and bond terms directly on your target institute's official admission page.

Beyond the degree

Higher studies

An M.Tech via GATE at an IIT or IISc is the standard route for students aiming at research roles or a stronger ISRO/DRDO application. An MS abroad — particularly in the US, Germany or France — is common for students targeting advanced propulsion or aerodynamics research at global aerospace companies. A PhD is the typical path for research scientist roles within ISRO or academic institutions.

Honest take

Advantages and disadvantages

Advantages

  • Genuinely prestigious, high-impact work — space missions, fighter jets, national defence systems.
  • India's updated space policy has opened real private-sector growth beyond just government roles.
  • Strong government pay scale and exceptional job security at ISRO/DRDO/HAL for those who clear the entrance route.

Disadvantages

  • India's private aerospace job market remains genuinely small compared to CSE or Mechanical.
  • GATE or a government exam (ICRB/SET) is close to essential for the best-paying roles, adding years of extra preparation.
  • Jobs are geographically concentrated in Bengaluru, Hyderabad and Thiruvananthapuram, limiting flexibility.

Common myths

Myths vs facts

MYTH Aerospace engineering guarantees a job at ISRO. ↓
FACT

ISRO recruitment is highly competitive with limited seats through ICRB. A large share of aerospace graduates work in private aviation, manufacturing or general engineering roles instead.

MYTH Aerospace engineering pays as well as CSE straight out of college. ↓
FACT

Private-sector fresher pay in aerospace is often modest (₹3-5 LPA) unless you land a government/PSU role via GATE, ICRB or SET — the field's best pay comes with genuinely extra competitive preparation, not automatically.

MYTH Aerospace engineering is only about building rockets or fighter jets. ↓
FACT

The field also covers commercial aviation, aircraft maintenance (MRO), drones/UAVs, and increasingly private space startups — a much broader set of options than just ISRO or HAL.
