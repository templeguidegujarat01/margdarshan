Overview

What is BAMS?

BAMS (Bachelor of Ayurvedic Medicine and Surgery) is a professional degree in Ayurveda, India's traditional system of medicine, combining Ayurvedic principles with modern medical science subjects like anatomy and physiology to diagnose, prevent and treat disease. It's regulated by the National Commission for Indian System of Medicine (NCISM) under the Ministry of AYUSH.

The field's standing has genuinely shifted in recent years. Regulatory reform now permits 58 authorised surgical procedures for MS Ayurveda postgraduates, the National AYUSH Mission and Ayushman Bharat's AYUSH Wellness Centres are creating real government positions, and the global Ayurveda and wellness market is projected to cross $14.9 billion by 2030. What hasn't changed is an honest, important limit: BAMS does not grant full modern-medicine (allopathic) practice rights in India.

Fit check

Who should choose BAMS

BAMS is a good fit if you...

  • Have genuine interest in Ayurveda and natural healing — not just choosing it because the NEET cutoff is lower.
  • Want a licensed medical career with a considerably more accessible entrance requirement than MBBS.
  • Are interested in India's growing wellness, spa tourism and Ayurvedic pharmaceutical industry as a career direction.

Think twice if you...

  • Specifically want to practise modern/allopathic medicine — BAMS does not grant this right in India, with narrow rural exceptions.
  • Are choosing it purely as an "easier NEET" fallback without genuine interest in Ayurveda itself.
  • Expect a BAMS degree to be automatically recognised as a medical licence abroad — recognition varies significantly by country.
Eligibility

Who can apply for BAMS

  • Class 12 pass with Physics, Chemistry and Biology (PCB) — under updated NCISM 2025-26 regulations, no minimum PCB aggregate is mandated beyond a Class 12 pass.
  • A qualifying score in NEET-UG is mandatory — the qualifying floor is just the 50th percentile for General category (40th for SC/ST/OBC).
  • This makes BAMS one of the more genuinely accessible NEET-based medical routes, though a safe government-college score is still considerably higher, typically 550-650 marks.

Genuinely affordable at government institutes

Fees & scholarships

Government BAMS colleges are among the most affordable medical-adjacent degrees available: annual fees commonly range from ₹10,000-70,000, meaning a full 5-year total of roughly ₹50,000-3.5 lakh. AIIA Delhi — described as the "AIIMS of Ayurveda" — charges approximately ₹35,000 per year. Private colleges are considerably more expensive, with annual fees commonly ₹1-4.5 lakh, totalling roughly ₹5-22 lakh over the full course. Always confirm current fees directly on your target college's official admission page.

The clearest lever for higher pay and scope

Higher studies

MD/MS Ayurveda, a 3-year postgraduate specialisation accessed via NEET-PG for AYUSH courses, is the standard next step for meaningful earning growth — tracks include Kayachikitsa (Internal Medicine), Shalya Tantra (Surgery, now including 58 authorised procedures), Prasuti Tantra (Obstetrics & Gynaecology), Shalakya Tantra (ENT/Ophthalmology) and Panchakarma. Some graduates instead pursue research through the Central Council for Research in Ayurvedic Sciences (CCRAS), or complementary diplomas in areas like Medical Laboratory Technology or Optometry to broaden their employability in wellness and diagnostic settings.

Honest take

Advantages and disadvantages

Advantages

  • Genuinely transformed via real regulatory reform (58 authorised surgical procedures for MS Ayurveda graduates) and a strong government AYUSH job push.
  • Considerably more accessible NEET cutoff than MBBS or BDS while still being a licensed medical degree.
  • Riding a genuine global wellness/Ayurveda market boom, with real international consulting opportunities.

Disadvantages

  • Does not grant full allopathic/modern-medicine practice rights in India — a genuine, important limitation to understand before enrolling.
  • International recognition varies significantly by country, with most requiring additional certification.
  • Fresher salaries in employed roles are modest, similar to BDS, with real income growth requiring practice-building time or a government AYUSH post.

Common myths

Myths vs facts

Myth: BAMS graduates can practise modern/allopathic medicine like an MBBS doctor.

Fact: BAMS does not grant full allopathic practice rights in India — some states allow limited prescribing in specific rural PHC contexts, but this is not equivalent to an MBBS doctor's scope of practice.

Myth: BAMS is only a fallback for students who can't get MBBS or BDS.

Fact: Real regulatory reforms (58 authorised surgical procedures for MS Ayurveda graduates), a strong government AYUSH job push, and a booming global wellness market have made BAMS a genuine primary career choice for students who are actually interested in Ayurveda.

Myth: An Ayurvedic degree from India is automatically recognised for practice abroad.

Fact: International recognition varies significantly by country; most require additional certification or licensing exams, even though Ayurveda itself is gaining global popularity as a wellness practice.
