Overview

What is the Turner trade?

  • ITI Turner is a 2-year, NCVT/DGT-affiliated trade under the Craftsman Training Scheme, training students specifically in lathe machine operation — turning, facing, threading and boring metal stock into precise cylindrical parts.
  • Where Machinist covers multiple machine tools, Turner goes deep on one: the lathe.
  • Training is heavily hands-on, working directly on lathes to produce parts to exact tolerance, combined with trade theory, engineering drawing and workshop calculation, following the standard Craftsmen Training Scheme (CTS) structure.

  • Turning skills also transfer well into modern Computer Numerical Control (CNC) turning centre operation.
Fit check

Who should choose this trade

✔ Ideal for you if…

  • Want to specialise deeply in one precise machining skill rather than broader machine-shop work.
  • Enjoy exacting, hands-on work with clear, measurable precision outcomes.
  • Are interested in eventually moving into CNC turning centre operation.

✖ Reconsider if…

  • Want broader exposure across multiple machine types — Machinist offers wider versatility.
  • Are uncomfortable with repetitive, precision-focused tasks requiring sustained concentration.
  • Prefer general assembly/maintenance work over specialised machine operation — Fitter fits better.

Beyond this trade

Higher studies & progression

Many institutes allow lateral entry into Diploma in Mechanical/Production Engineering for ITI Turner holders, typically into a later year, though rules vary by state and institute. A dedicated CNC turning operator certification is a genuinely valuable and common next step, since manual lathe skills transfer well into CNC systems and open access to higher-paying, more automated roles. Railways and defence technical recruitment exams are also common direct paths.

Honest take

Advantages and disadvantages

Advantages

  • Deep specialisation in a genuinely valuable, precise machining skill.
  • Skills transfer smoothly into higher-paying CNC turning centre roles.
  • Strong government/PSU and defence technical recruitment access.

Disadvantages

  • Narrower scope than Machinist — focused specifically on lathe/turning work.
  • Repetitive, precision-focused work that requires sustained concentration.
  • Without CNC upskilling, manual lathe roles may see slower pay growth over time.
