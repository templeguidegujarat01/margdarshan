JEE Advanced — explained simply

The basics

What is JEE Advanced?

JEE Advanced is a national-level entrance exam used specifically for admission to the Indian Institutes of Technology (IITs). Unlike JEE Main, which anyone eligible can register for directly, JEE Advanced is only open to a limited number of top-ranking candidates who first qualify through JEE Main. It's conducted by one of the IITs each year, on a rotating basis, under the overall coordination of the JEE Apex Board.

Common confusion

JEE Advanced vs. JEE Main

JEE Main

Open exam anyone eligible can register for. Used for NITs, IIITs and GFTIs. Also the qualifying stage for JEE Advanced eligibility.

JEE Advanced

Only open to a limited number of top JEE Main rankers each year. Used specifically for IIT admission. Requires separate registration after qualifying JEE Main.

Basic requirements

Eligibility, at a stable level

To be eligible for JEE Advanced, you must first rank within the limited group of top qualifiers in JEE Main that year — the exact cut-off group size is decided fresh each year and isn't a fixed number you can rely on in advance. Beyond that, candidates typically also need to meet conditions on Class 12 performance and the number of years since passing Class 12.

Because these specific conditions (cut-off group size, marks requirements, age-related rules and permitted attempts) are set in each year's official information bulletin and do change, always verify current-year eligibility on the official JEE Advanced website rather than assuming last year's rules still apply.

How the exam works

Exam pattern — the stable structure

JEE Advanced is a computer-based test conducted in two compulsory papers (Paper 1 and Paper 2), both of which most candidates need to attempt. It covers Physics, Chemistry and Mathematics at a level generally considered more conceptually demanding than JEE Main, with a strong emphasis on problem-solving rather than direct formula recall.

Specific details such as the number of questions, marking scheme, exact duration and session dates are published fresh in each year's official bulletin by that year's organising IIT — treat any specific figures elsewhere as something to confirm officially.

Once you're done

What happens after the exam?

After results, admission to the IITs happens through a centralised counselling and seat-allotment process (typically the same joint counselling system used for JEE Main-based institutes). Your JEE Advanced rank, along with your choice of branch and institute preferences, determines which IIT and programme you're offered.
