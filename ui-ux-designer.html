Overview

What does a UI/UX Designer actually do?

  • UI/UX Design covers how digital products look and how they work for the people using them.
  • UI (User Interface) is what you see — buttons, colors, layout, typography.
  • UX (User Experience) is what you feel — research, flow, and whether the product is genuinely easy and satisfying to use.
Read moreRead less↓
  • The role has genuinely broadened by 2026: designers are increasingly expected to know motion design basics, accessibility standards (WCAG), and how to work alongside AI-assisted design tools like Figma Artificial Intelligence (AI), Galileo and Uizard — as productivity multipliers, not replacements for design judgment.
  • India is reported to need over 80,000 additional UI/UX designers by 2027, and AI adoption is reported to be increasing designer productivity and demand rather than shrinking the role.
Fit check

Who should aim for a UI/UX Designer career

✔ Ideal for you if…

  • Enjoy both creative visual work and structured, research-driven problem-solving.
  • Want a field where a strong, self-built portfolio can genuinely compete with formal design-school credentials.
  • Are willing to learn to speak in business metrics, not just aesthetics, to unlock the field's higher pay bands.

✖ Reconsider if…

  • Want to focus only on making things "look nice" without ever doing research — the field's higher-paying roles specifically require both.
  • Dislike documenting and presenting your design decisions — that's exactly what separates portfolios that get interviews from ones that don't.
  • Expect a design degree alone, without a strong portfolio, to be sufficient.
Two genuinely valid routes

Routes into a UI/UX Design career

  • ✓Formal design education — institutes like National Institute of Design (NID) or Srishti offer the strongest campus placement starting point, generally admitted via design-specific entrance exams.
  • ✓Self-taught Figma + documented portfolio — a widely accepted, common path, especially into startups and freelance work; open to any degree or stream.
  • ✓No Physics, Chemistry, Mathematics (PCM) or Joint Entrance Examination (JEE) is required for either route.
Step-by-step

Your path to becoming a UI/UX Designer

  1. 1

    Design fundamentals

    Color theory, typography, layout and visual hierarchy.

  2. 2

    Learn Figma deeply

    Auto-layout, components, variables, prototyping — used by roughly 95% of Indian product companies.

  3. 3

    Basic UX research methods

    User interviews and usability testing, even at a small scale.

  4. 4

    Practice on real products

    Redesign real apps or websites, documenting your process along the way.

  5. 5

    Build 3-4 documented case studies

    Problem → research → iteration → outcome — the format that actually gets interview callbacks.

Two very different cost profiles

Cost of each route

A formal design education at an institute like NID or Srishti is a multi-lakh, multi-year commitment, similar in scale to any other specialised undergraduate degree — check each institute's current fee structure directly. The self-taught route is genuinely far cheaper: Figma itself is free to learn, and countless free tutorials cover the fundamentals. Guided, structured courses, for students who want a faster, more organised path, commonly cost ₹20,000-1,50,000 — always verify a course's actual placement outcomes before enrolling.

Skills over further degrees, for most

Career growth paths

Further formal education isn't the typical growth lever if you already have a strong portfolio — deepening UX research skills, mastering design systems, or moving into full Product Design or Design Leadership roles are reported as the clearest paths to higher pay. The mid-to-senior level is reported to be significantly undersupplied relative to demand, since the pool of designers who can do proper UX research, build design systems, and present data-backed decisions to stakeholders is much smaller than the pool of entry-level UI designers. A postgraduate design programme at NID or Srishti remains a valid option for those wanting formal credentials specifically.

Honest take

Advantages and disadvantages

Advantages

  • Genuinely accessible via a strong self-built portfolio, competing meaningfully with formal design-school credentials.
  • India needs a reported 80,000+ additional UI/UX designers by 2027, with AI tools increasing rather than reducing demand.
  • Flexible career path opening into product management, UX research, or design leadership over time.

Disadvantages

  • Entry-level "UI Designer" roles at most non-product companies pay meaningfully less than the higher senior/product-design numbers you'll see quoted online — manage expectations at the fresher stage.
  • A portfolio of just visuals without documented case studies (research/iteration/outcome) is a real, common mistake that limits interview callbacks.
  • The mid-to-senior skill bar (UX research, design systems, data-driven presentation) is genuinely higher than many beginners expect.
Common myths

Myths vs facts

MYTH UI, UX and Product Designer are basically the same job, just different names. ↓
FACT

These titles get used interchangeably in job postings, but they carry genuinely different responsibilities and pay differently — UX and Product Designer roles typically pay 15-25% more than a plain UI-only role at the same experience level.

MYTH A design degree from NID or a top design school is required to become a UI/UX designer. ↓
FACT

Many startups and product companies now prioritise portfolio quality over college degrees — self-taught designers with a strong, documented portfolio are hired directly and freelance successfully.

MYTH AI design tools like Figma AI or Galileo are going to replace UI/UX designers. ↓
FACT

These tools are reported to be accelerating designer productivity, not replacing the role — product thinking, user empathy and strategic design judgment remain fundamentally human skills that AI tools don't replicate.
