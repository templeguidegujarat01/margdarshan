Overview

What is the Plumber trade?

  • ITI Plumber is a shorter, NCVT/DGT-affiliated trade — typically around 1 year — under the Craftsman Training Scheme, training students in water supply pipeline installation, drainage systems, pipefitting techniques, and repair of plumbing fixtures.
  • It's accessible to students from Class 8 pass in many states, in addition to the more common Class 10 entry point.
  • Training is heavily hands-on — cutting, joining and installing pipes, fixing leaks, and understanding water supply and drainage layouts — combined with trade theory and safety practices, following the standard Craftsmen Training Scheme (CTS) structure.
Read more
  • Plumbing stands out among ITI trades for its reliably strong, hyper-local self-employment potential, since every building needs plumbing work that can't be outsourced or automated.
Fit check

Who should choose this trade

✔ Ideal for you if…

  • Want a fast, accessible trade with genuinely strong self-employment potential.
  • Are comfortable with hands-on, sometimes physically demanding practical work.
  • Want steady, reliable local demand that isn't easily affected by economic cycles or automation.

✖ Reconsider if…

  • Are uncomfortable with physically demanding work in tight or awkward spaces.
  • Prefer predictable, office-based work over service calls and on-site jobs.
  • Want a technically deep specialisation — plumbing is practical and broad rather than highly technical.

Beyond this trade

Higher studies & progression

Plumbing doesn't typically follow a formal diploma lateral-entry path the way engineering trades do; progression is largely experience and reputation-driven. Many plumbers add valuable skills through short courses in water purifier/RO system installation, solar water heater installation, or specialised bathroom fitting to expand their service offerings and income, given rising household demand for these add-on services.

Honest take

Advantages and disadvantages

Advantages

  • One of the most reliably self-employable ITI trades, with constant local demand.
  • Very accessible entry point, with Class 8 eligibility in many states.
  • Not easily outsourced, automated, or affected by economic downturns.

Disadvantages

  • Physically demanding work, often in tight or awkward spaces.
  • Salaried entry-level pay is modest; real income growth mostly comes through going independent.
  • No formal higher-education progression path like more technical ITI trades.
