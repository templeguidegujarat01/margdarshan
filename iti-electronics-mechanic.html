Overview

What is the Electronics Mechanic trade?

  • ITI Electronics Mechanic is a 2-year, NCVT/DGT-affiliated trade under the Craftsman Training Scheme, training students in electronic circuit theory, component identification, fault diagnosis and repair of consumer electronics — televisions, appliances, audio equipment, and basic circuit boards.
  • It works with lower-voltage electronic circuits, distinct from the higher-current electrical systems covered in the Electrician trade.
  • Training is heavily hands-on, working directly with real devices and test equipment to diagnose and fix faults, combined with trade theory and circuit fundamentals, following the standard Craftsmen Training Scheme (CTS) structure.

  • As electronics increasingly integrate into everyday products (smart appliances, IoT devices), this trade's relevance continues to grow, though specific niches like smartphone repair typically need additional specialised training.
Fit check

Who should choose this trade

✔ Ideal for you if…

  • Enjoy troubleshooting and diagnosing faults in electronic devices.
  • Want a trade that stays relevant as electronics become more common in everyday products.
  • Are interested in eventually specialising further, such as in mobile repair or IoT devices.

✖ Reconsider if…

  • Want to work specifically with power distribution and higher-current wiring — Electrician fits better.
  • Expect the base trade alone to fully cover modern smartphone repair — that typically needs further training.
  • Are uncomfortable with detailed, precision-focused troubleshooting work.

Beyond this trade

Higher studies & progression

Many institutes allow lateral entry into Diploma in Electronics & Communication Engineering for ITI Electronics Mechanic holders, typically into a later year, though rules vary by state and institute. Additional specialised training in mobile phone repair, IoT devices, or embedded systems is a genuinely valuable next step given the growing connected-device market, and can meaningfully boost both job options and self-employment potential.

Honest take

Advantages and disadvantages

Advantages

  • Stays relevant as electronics become more embedded in everyday consumer products.
  • Genuine self-employment potential through opening your own repair shop.
  • Clear specialisation paths (mobile, IoT) that can boost pay significantly.

Disadvantages

  • Modern smartphone/high-tech repair typically requires additional specialised training beyond the base trade.
  • Some traditional consumer electronics repair demand has shifted with changing "repair vs replace" consumer habits.
  • Entry-level private-sector pay can be modest without apprenticeship experience or specialisation.
