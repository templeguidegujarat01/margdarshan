Interior Design — Shaping How Spaces Work & Feel

8 min read · Degree or diploma route · Open after Class 12, any stream

Interior Design plans how the inside of a space functions and feels — layout, materials, lighting, furniture and finishes — for homes, offices, retail and hospitality projects. It's a genuinely practical, client-facing design career, distinct from Architecture, with real growth as India's real estate and hospitality sectors expand. This guide covers eligibility, the entrance process, and where it leads.

Overview

What is Interior Design?

  • Interior Design is the practice of planning and shaping the interior of a space — how rooms flow, what materials and finishes are used, how furniture is arranged, and how lighting affects mood and function — for homes, offices, retail stores, restaurants and hotels.
  • It blends creative vision with practical knowledge of materials, ergonomics, budgets and building codes.
  • It's offered as a B.Des (Bachelor of Design) at institutes like NID and several private design schools, or as shorter diploma programmes at institutes like Pearl Academy, JD Institute of Fashion Technology, or CEPT University's design offerings.
Fit check

Who should choose Interior Design

✔ Ideal for you if…

  • Enjoy thinking about how spaces feel and function, not just how they look in a photo.
  • Are comfortable balancing creative vision with practical constraints — budgets, building codes and client needs.
  • Are willing to learn technical tools like AutoCAD alongside creative design skills.

✖ Reconsider if…

  • Want to design entire building structures — that's Architecture, a separate, more technical, licensed profession.
  • Dislike direct client interaction — managing client expectations and revisions is a constant part of the work.
  • Expect quick, glamorous project turnarounds — real projects often involve long timelines, vendor coordination and site visits.
Eligibility

Who can apply for Interior Design

  • ✓Class 12 pass (10+2) from any stream, typically with a minimum aggregate around 50%, varying by institute.
  • ✓Design aptitude entrance exams like NID DAT, UCEED, or NATA (also used for architecture) are commonly accepted, depending on the institute.
  • ✓No prior design background is compulsory, though a basic sketching/design portfolio genuinely helps at the interview stage.

Wide range by institute

Fees & scholarships

NID's design programmes are typically in the range of ₹2.5-3.5 lakh per year, being a public institute with government support. Private design schools can charge ₹2-5 lakh per year, while shorter diploma courses at smaller institutes are more affordable, often ₹50,000-1.5 lakh total. Merit-based scholarships are available at select institutes. Always confirm current fees directly on the official institute website.

Honest take

Advantages and disadvantages

Advantages

  • Real, growing demand from India's expanding real estate, hospitality and retail sectors.
  • Genuinely creative, tangible work with visible, lasting results.
  • Strong potential for independent consulting once you've built experience and a client base.

Disadvantages

  • Entry-level pay at smaller firms can be modest relative to the technical and creative skill required.
  • Projects can involve long timelines, site visits and vendor coordination beyond just design work.
  • Independent consulting income can be inconsistent, especially before building a strong reputation.
Common myths

Myths vs facts

MYTH Interior Designers just pick colours and furniture. ↓
FACT

The role involves technical space planning, budget management, vendor coordination, lighting design and understanding building codes — far more than aesthetic choices alone.

MYTH Interior Design and Architecture are basically the same career. ↓
FACT

They're genuinely distinct — Architecture is a licensed profession dealing with building structure and engineering, while Interior Design shapes the interior of existing spaces without the same licensing requirement.

MYTH You need an engineering background to study Interior Design. ↓
FACT

It's open to students from any Class 12 stream — it's a creative design discipline, not a core engineering one, though basic spatial reasoning genuinely helps.
