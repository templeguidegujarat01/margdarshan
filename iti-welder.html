Overview

What is the Welder trade?

  • ITI Welder is a shorter, NCVT/DGT-affiliated trade — typically around 1 year — under the Craftsman Training Scheme, training students in metal joining techniques including arc welding (SMAW) and gas welding, with growing exposure to MIG/TIG processes at better-equipped institutes.
  • It's a fast, practical trade with genuinely strong demand across construction, manufacturing and fabrication.
  • Training is heavily hands-on, with substantial time spent actually welding test joints and structures, alongside trade theory and strict safety practices, following the standard Craftsmen Training Scheme (CTS) structure.
Read moreRead less↓
  • Skilled welders also have real, though not automatic, opportunities in international markets including the Gulf, subject to additional certification requirements.
Fit check

Who should choose this trade

✔ Ideal for you if…

  • Enjoy hands-on fabrication work and are steady, focused and detail-oriented.
  • Want a fast trade with strong construction and manufacturing demand.
  • Are open to pursuing further certification for international/Gulf job opportunities.

✖ Reconsider if…

  • Are uncomfortable with heat, sparks and sustained protective gear use.
  • Expect the base ITI certificate alone to guarantee overseas job placement — it doesn't.
  • Prefer precision machine operation over manual fabrication — Machinist or Turner may suit better.

Beyond this trade

Higher studies & progression

Many welders pursue internationally recognised welding certifications (following standards used globally, such as AWS-aligned coding) after the ITI trade, which genuinely opens doors to higher-paying domestic and overseas roles, particularly in shipbuilding, oil & gas and Gulf construction markets. Some institutes also allow lateral entry into Diploma in Mechanical Engineering. Specialised training in pipe welding or structural welding can further boost employability and pay.

Honest take

Advantages and disadvantages

Advantages

  • Fast, in-demand trade with strong construction and manufacturing job scope.
  • Genuine, well-established international/Gulf opportunities for certified welders.
  • Real self-employment/contracting potential with experience.

Disadvantages

  • Physically demanding, with real heat/spark exposure requiring consistent safety discipline.
  • International job placement requires additional certification — not automatic with the base ITI trade.
  • MIG/TIG equipment exposure varies significantly by institute quality.
