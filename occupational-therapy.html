Overview

What is Occupational Therapy?

Occupational Therapy (OT) is a healthcare profession that helps people regain independence in their daily lives after illness, injury, disability or developmental conditions. Occupational Therapists work with children, adults and elderly patients to improve their ability to perform everyday activities — eating, dressing, writing, studying, working and self-care — with the goal of restoring independence and quality of life.

What's genuinely distinctive about this field's 2026 demand story is that it's driven by three separate, real forces simultaneously: rising awareness of autism, ADHD and developmental disorders (fuelling growth in paediatric OT, the field's fastest-growing segment), real need for stroke and neurological rehabilitation, and India's genuinely ageing population requiring geriatric functional support. Reported demand growth of 25% over the past three years reflects all three trends compounding together, not a single hype-driven cause.

Fit check

Who should choose Occupational Therapy

Occupational Therapy is a good fit if you...

  • Are genuinely patient and empathetic, interested in the connection between ability and daily functioning.
  • Are drawn specifically to paediatric development, neuro-rehabilitation, or geriatric care — not just "healthcare in general."
  • Are comfortable with a still-maturing national regulatory framework, similar to Physiotherapy's.

Think twice if you...

  • Expect a fixed, narrow clinical role — OT genuinely spans very different populations, requiring real range.
  • Want to skip specialisation — the field's most in-demand, best-paid roles are specifically paediatric or neuro-focused.
  • Dislike emotionally engaged, relationship-based therapeutic work.
Eligibility

Who can apply for Occupational Therapy

  • Class 12 pass with Physics, Chemistry and Biology (PCB), or PCMB at some institutions — Mathematics is accepted in place of Biology at a number of colleges.
  • Minimum aggregate typically in the 45-50% range, varying by institute.
  • NEET is not required — admission is via CUET, university-specific tests, state-level admissions, or direct merit.

Comparatively affordable healthcare route

Fees & scholarships

Government and university-affiliated colleges typically offer the most affordable annual fees, while private colleges and specialised rehabilitation institutes vary more widely based on facilities, clinical partnerships and city. Common myths

Myths vs facts

Myth: Occupational Therapy and Physiotherapy are basically the same job.

Fact: They genuinely differ in focus — Physiotherapy centres on restoring movement and physical function through exercise and manual therapy, while Occupational Therapy focuses specifically on helping patients perform daily living activities and regain functional independence, often working with very different patient populations including children with developmental disorders.

Myth: Occupational Therapy is only for elderly or injured patients.

Fact: Paediatric OT — working with children who have autism, ADHD, sensory processing difficulties or developmental delays — is reported as the field's fastest-growing segment, alongside genuinely distinct work in stroke rehabilitation and mental health support.

Myth: OT salaries in India are uniformly low.

Fact: While fresher pay is genuinely modest (₹3-5 LPA), specialised practitioners in neuro or paediatric rehabilitation reach ₹7-10 LPA by mid-career, and senior consultants or private clinic owners are reported to reach ₹12-20+ LPA.
