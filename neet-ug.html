The basics

What is NEET-UG, and why does it exist?

NEET-UG (National Eligibility cum Entrance Test — Undergraduate) is a national-level entrance exam conducted by the National Testing Agency (NTA) for admission to undergraduate medical and allied medical courses in India. Before NEET, different states and colleges ran separate entrance tests, which meant students often had to prepare for and travel to multiple exams. NEET replaced that with one common test used almost everywhere in the country.

NEET-UG is used for admission to MBBS, BDS, AYUSH courses (such as BAMS and BHMS), BVSc & AH, and BSc Nursing programmes, across both government and private medical colleges.

Is this for you?

Who needs to take NEET-UG?

Any student who wants to study medicine or an allied medical/health degree in India — this covers a wider range of careers than just "becoming a doctor."

You should consider it if:

You're in or have finished Class 12 with Physics, Chemistry and Biology, and you're interested in medicine, dentistry, Ayurveda/Homeopathy, veterinary science or nursing.

You probably don't need it if:

You're aiming for engineering (which uses JEE Main/Advanced instead), or fields like pharmacy admission through certain routes and many allied-health diploma/paramedical courses, which commonly use separate, non-NEET admission processes — always confirm the specific course's own admission route.

Basic requirements

Eligibility, at a stable level

The standard requirement is having passed or currently appearing for Class 12 (or an equivalent qualification) with Physics, Chemistry and Biology as compulsory subjects. English is also typically required as a subject of study.

Exact details like minimum qualifying marks/percentile, age-related conditions, number of attempts allowed, and category-wise relaxations are set fresh in each year's official information bulletin and can change. Always verify current-year eligibility on the official NEET website before assuming you qualify.

How the exam works

Exam pattern — the stable structure

NEET-UG has traditionally been conducted as a single pen-and-paper (offline) test covering Physics, Chemistry and Biology (Botany and Zoology), unlike JEE Main which is computer-based. It's held once a year, on a single date, at exam centres across India and a small number of centres abroad.

Specific details such as the exact number of questions, marking scheme and exam duration are published fresh in each year's official notification — and the exam mode itself has been a subject of ongoing discussion in some years, so always confirm the current mode and pattern via the official NEET notification rather than assuming it matches a previous year.

Once you're done

What happens after the exam?

After results are declared, admission happens through a separate counselling process rather than automatically. All-India quota and central/deemed university seats are generally allocated through the Medical Counselling Committee (MCC), while state quota seats and most state government/private college seats go through each state's own counselling authority. Your rank, category, and the specific quota/state rules together determine which college and course you're offered — qualifying the exam alone does not guarantee a seat, particularly for MBBS, where seats are limited relative to the number of applicants.

Getting ready

How students usually prepare

NEET-UG preparation is one of the largest coaching sectors in India, typically built around Class 11-12 Physics, Chemistry and Biology, with a heavy emphasis on NCERT-level concepts, extensive practice and repeated revision. Some students prepare through long-term classroom coaching (often starting from Class 11), others through online courses, crash courses or self-study — there's no single required path, though NEET's syllabus depth generally rewards sustained, structured preparation over last-minute cramming.

Official information

Always confirm details officially

NEET-UG is conducted by the National Testing Agency (NTA). For current dates, eligibility, fees, syllabus and the official information bulletin, always check the official website directly rather than relying on any third-party source, including this page.
