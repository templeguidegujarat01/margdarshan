Overview

What is the Electrician trade?

  • ITI Electrician is a 2-year, NCVT/DGT-affiliated trade under the Craftsman Training Scheme, training students in electrical wiring, motor winding and repair, electrical machines, control panels, and safety practices.
  • It's one of the most established and widely recognised ITI trades, with steady demand across households, industries, construction and government infrastructure.
  • Students spend substantial time in the workshop — actually wiring circuits, handling motors and testing equipment — alongside trade theory, workplace calculation, and employability skills, following the standard Craftsmen Training Scheme (CTS) course structure.
Fit check

Who should choose this trade

✔ Ideal for you if…

  • Are genuinely interested in electrical systems, wiring and how machines are powered.
  • Want a widely recognised, in-demand trade with genuine self-employment potential.
  • Are comfortable following strict safety protocols around live electrical work.

✖ Reconsider if…

  • Want to independently run electrical contracting work immediately — that needs the additional state license discussed below.
  • Are uncomfortable around electrical hazards, even with safety training.
  • Prefer a shorter, quicker trade — Wireman offers a faster (though narrower) alternative.

Beyond this trade

Higher studies & progression

Many institutes allow lateral entry into Diploma in Electrical Engineering for ITI Electrician holders, typically into a later year, though rules vary by state and institute. The Electrical Contractor/Supervisor License (issued by your state Electrical Inspectorate after meeting experience requirements and passing a state exam) is the key credential for independent contracting work. Further specialisation via certifications in industrial automation, solar/renewable energy systems, or advanced motor control can also strengthen your profile.

Honest take

Advantages and disadvantages

Advantages

  • One of the most widely recognised, consistently in-demand ITI trades across India.
  • Genuine self-employment potential after gaining the required license and experience.
  • Strong government technical job access via railways, DISCOMs and Public Sector Undertaking (PSU) recruitment exams.

Disadvantages

  • Involves real electrical hazards, requiring consistent safety discipline.
  • Independent contracting requires an additional state license, not granted automatically with the ITI certificate.
  • Entry-level private-sector pay can be modest without apprenticeship experience.
