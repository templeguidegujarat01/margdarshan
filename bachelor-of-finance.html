Bachelor of Finance — BFM (Bachelor of Financial Markets)

9 min read · Mumbai University specialised degree · Open after Class 12, any stream

When Indian students search "Bachelor of Finance," what they usually find is BFM — Bachelor of Financial Markets, a specialised 3-year undergraduate degree introduced by the University of Mumbai in 2008-09. Unlike a general B.Com, BFM is built entirely around financial markets, equity research, derivatives and investment banking from the very first year. This guide explains exactly what BFM involves, and when it makes more sense than a general commerce degree.

Overview

What is Bachelor of Finance (BFM)?

Bachelor of Financial Markets (BFM) is a 3-year specialised undergraduate degree covering financial markets, equity research, derivatives, risk management, investment banking and financial modelling — subjects a general B.Com only touches briefly, if at all. It was introduced by the University of Mumbai and remains concentrated at its affiliated colleges, making it the closest thing India has to a dedicated "Bachelor of Finance" degree.

Where B.Com builds a broad foundation across accounting, taxation and business law, BFM goes narrow and deep into how stock markets, banks and investment firms actually function — closer in spirit to a specialised finance-track degree than to a general commerce one.

Fit check

Who should choose BFM

BFM is a good fit if you...

  • Already know you want a career in stock markets, investment banking, or financial analysis, not general commerce.
  • Are based in or near Mumbai, where nearly all BFM colleges are concentrated.
  • Plan to pursue CFA or FRM afterward, since the syllabus overlaps meaningfully with both.

Think twice if you...

  • Are based outside Maharashtra — BFM colleges are hard to find elsewhere; B.Com with a finance elective is more accessible.
  • Are still unsure between accounting, taxation, general business and finance — B.Com keeps more doors open at once.
  • Want a nationally recognisable degree name for jobs or further study outside India — B.Com or a Bachelor of Finance-equivalent degree abroad has broader recognition.
Eligibility

Who can apply for BFM

  • Class 12 pass (10+2) from a recognised board, any stream — though a Commerce, Economics, Maths or Statistics background helps with the syllabus.
  • Minimum aggregate typically 45-50%, varying by college.
  • Some colleges expect Mathematics at Class 12 and hold their own entrance test; several Mumbai University-affiliated colleges instead admit directly on merit.

Generally affordable

Fees & scholarships

BFM is one of the more affordable specialised commerce degrees at Mumbai University-affiliated colleges — some report a total tuition fee for the full 3-year programme as low as roughly ₹58,000, broadly similar to B.Com and BMS at equivalent institution types. Private and autonomous colleges can charge more, and fees are revised periodically, so always confirm current figures directly on your target college's official admission page. Maharashtra state government scholarships and college-specific fee concessions are available for eligible students.

Beyond the degree

Higher studies

BFM's syllabus overlaps meaningfully with the CFA curriculum, making it a natural stepping stone toward the CFA charter or the FRM certification for students aiming at investment analysis or risk management roles. Many graduates also pursue an MBA in Finance via CAT, XAT or a university-specific entrance test, or international qualifications like CPA or ACCA depending on their target industry.

Honest take

Advantages and disadvantages

Advantages

  • A genuinely specialised finance curriculum — equity research, derivatives and financial modelling — that's rare to see this early at undergraduate level.
  • Strong overlap with CFA and FRM syllabi, giving a head start on those certifications.
  • Generally affordable at Mumbai University-affiliated colleges, close to India's largest financial services hub.

Disadvantages

  • Concentrated almost entirely in Maharashtra — far less accessible than B.Com if you're elsewhere in India.
  • Narrower than B.Com — less useful if you later want to pivot into accounting, taxation or general management.
  • On its own, rarely leads to the most competitive investment banking roles without a follow-on certification like CFA or an MBA.

Common myths

Myths vs facts

Myth: BFM is just B.Com with an extra Finance subject.

Fact: BFM is a separate, specialised programme with a markets-focused syllabus — equity research, derivatives, financial modelling — that a standard B.Com only touches briefly, if at all.

Myth: BFM is available across India, just like B.Com.

Fact: BFM is predominantly a University of Mumbai degree. Students outside Maharashtra will find far fewer colleges offering it and should check availability carefully before planning around it.

Myth: A BFM degree guarantees a stock market or investment banking job.

Fact: It builds a genuinely relevant foundation, but the most competitive markets and investment banking roles usually still require a strong college, a certification like CFA, or an MBA on top.
