Overview

What is the Surveyor trade?

ITI Surveyor is a shorter, NCVT/DGT-affiliated trade — typically around 1 year — under the Craftsman Training Scheme, training students in land measurement, mapping and levelling using survey instruments including chains, compasses, dumpy levels, theodolites, and increasingly, total stations and basic GPS-based tools.

Where Draughtsman Civil works mostly at a desk preparing drawings, Surveyors work primarily in the field, taking measurements on construction sites, land parcels and infrastructure projects that feed into planning, construction and legal land records.

Fit check

Who should choose this trade

This trade is a good fit if you...

  • Prefer active, outdoor field work over desk-based office tasks.
  • Are precise, detail-oriented and comfortable with measurement instruments.
  • Want genuine government land-record/PWD job access alongside private construction sector demand.

Think twice if you...

  • Prefer indoor, desk-based drafting work — Draughtsman Civil fits better.
  • Are uncomfortable working outdoors in varied weather and terrain conditions.
  • Expect the base trade to make you an independently licensed Land Surveyor for all official purposes without further certification.
Eligibility

Who can apply

  • Class 10 pass, generally with Science and Maths, though exact subject requirements vary slightly by state.
  • Minimum age typically 14 years at admission, varying by state.
  • Exact minimum marks and reservation norms vary by state — always confirm with your state's current ITI counselling notification.
Step-by-step

Your path through this trade

  1. 1

    Class 10 with Science & Maths

    Clear your state's ITI counselling process based on merit.

  2. 2

    ~1 year of trade training

    Survey theory combined with substantial hands-on field practice using survey instruments.

  3. 3

    NCVT trade exam & apprenticeship

    Clear the final trade exam, then take up a paid apprenticeship at a construction or survey firm.

  4. 4

    Job, government exam, or further study

    Join a construction firm as a Survey Assistant, apply for Revenue/PWD posts, or pursue a diploma.

Beyond this trade

Higher studies & progression

Many institutes allow lateral entry into Diploma in Civil Engineering for ITI Surveyor holders, typically into a later year, though rules vary by state and institute. Further certifications in total station operation, GPS surveying, or GIS (Geographic Information Systems) can meaningfully strengthen your profile and pay, since modern surveying increasingly relies on digital and satellite-based tools alongside traditional instruments.

Honest take

Advantages and disadvantages

Advantages

  • Active, field-based trade for students who don't want desk-only work.
  • Genuine government job access via Revenue Department and PWD recruitment.
  • Clear specialisation path toward higher-paying total station/GPS surveying roles.

Disadvantages

  • Outdoor field work in varied weather and terrain conditions.
  • Total station/GPS equipment exposure varies significantly by institute quality.
  • Certain official land survey roles may require additional departmental certification beyond the base trade.
