How we source our data

Our rules

Official sources only

Government statistics (BLS, O*NET, NCES, USCIS), exam owners (College Board, ACT, AAMC, NCBE, NCEES, NBME/FSMB) and licensing or accrediting bodies. Blogs and forums are never the source of a fact.

Copied, not calculated

Pay, outlook and counts are shown exactly as the source publishes them. We do not estimate places or years a source does not publish; those show as "Not published".

Dated and reviewed

Every figure carries its data period (for example, wages for {{ V.wage_ref }}). Rules that change often — immigration, exam formats, loan limits — are re-checked on a schedule.

Advice such as "Stand out" tips is Margdarshan's own guidance and is labelled that way. It is based on the data, but it is not a source fact.

How to read the numbers

  • Median payHalf of workers earned more and half earned less. It is less affected by a few very high salaries than the average (mean).
  • 10th / 25th / 75th / 90th percentile10% of workers earned less than the 10th percentile; 10% earned more than the 90th. The middle half sits between the 25th and 75th.
  • Job outlookBLS projection of the change in employment from 2025 to 2035. All jobs together are projected to grow {{ V.all_occupations_growth_pct }}%.
  • BLS group figuresSome BLS profiles cover several occupations (for example, software developers with QA analysts and testers). When the outlook or openings belong to the group, the page says so.
  • Jobs: two different countsSalary pages use OEWS, a survey of employers ({{ US.oews_summary.period }}). Outlook figures use the BLS projections, which also count self-employed workers. They are never mixed in one number.
  • Very high wagesWhen BLS shows a wage only as "equal to or greater than" a cap, we show it with a "+" sign.
