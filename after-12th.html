First, choose your stream

What your Class 12 stream generally opens up

Your stream narrows down the most straightforward next steps — it doesn't lock you into one single career. Here's what each stream typically leads to.

Science (PCM / PCB)

Mostly leads to a degree, usually followed by an entrance exam. PCM students typically look at engineering (via JEE-style exams) or other technical degrees. PCB students typically look at medical and life-science courses (via NEET-style exams) or allied health degrees. Some Science students also move into general degrees or professional commerce/finance courses that don't require a stream-specific exam.

MBBSCSECivil EngineeringNursing
Explore Science careers in detail

Commerce

Leads either to a regular degree (B.Com, BBA and similar) or directly into a professional qualification such as CA, CS or CMA, which are earned through the governing institute's own exams rather than a university degree. Both routes can also lead toward business, banking, government finance roles or further studies like an MBA.

CACSCMAB.Com
Explore Commerce careers in detail

Arts / Humanities

Leads to a wide range of degree and professional routes — law (via LL.B.), civil services and government administration, teaching (via B.Ed.), design, media and journalism, psychology, and social work, among others. Many Arts careers involve a degree first, followed by a specific entrance or professional exam depending on the field.

LawCivil ServicesDesignJournalism

Diploma / skill-first routes

Diploma (Polytechnic) programmes are usually associated with a shorter, skill-first route into engineering and technical trades, most commonly taken right after Class 10. If you're finishing Class 12 and want a faster, more hands-on route than a full degree, it's still worth exploring the Diploma hub — check a specific programme's own eligibility criteria for exact entry requirements after Class 12.

How it generally works

The general shape of most career paths

The exact steps differ by career — always check the specific guide — but most paths follow this general shape.

Class 12 (your stream)
↓
Choose a suitable course or career direction
↓
Complete the required qualification (degree or professional course)
↓
Entrance / professional / government exam, where the career requires one
↓
Career / job

Not every path includes every step — for example, some professional courses like CA don't need a separate entrance exam beyond the institute's own exams, while government roles often add a recruitment exam after your qualification is complete.

An important distinction

A "career" and a "government job" aren't always the same thing

It's easy to think of "government job" as one single path, but it usually works one of two ways. Sometimes you first complete a specific qualification — an engineering or medical degree, for example — and later appear for a government recruitment exam relevant to that qualification. Other times, a government exam has its own direct entrance route that doesn't require a specific degree first, and is instead open to graduates from any stream.

We already have detailed guides for a few government-linked career paths, such as UPSC Civil Services, NDA and CDS. A dedicated Government Careers hub — covering UPSC, GPSC, SSC, Banking, Railways, Defence and other exams in one organised place — is being built and will expand this section further. For now, explore the guides that already exist above, and check back as more are added.
