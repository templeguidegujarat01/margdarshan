Overview

What is Audiology & Speech Therapy?

BASLP (Bachelor of Audiology and Speech-Language Pathology) is a 4-year professional degree — 3 years of coursework plus a compulsory 1-year clinical internship — that trains graduates to diagnose and treat hearing problems and speech, language, and swallowing disorders across all age groups. It's regulated by the Rehabilitation Council of India (RCI), ensuring standardised education and professional practice, and RCI registration after graduation is required for clinical practice.

Here's what genuinely sets this field apart: the WHO reports over 1.5 billion people globally experience some form of hearing loss, projected to exceed 2.5 billion by 2050. In India specifically, an ageing population, rising industrial noise-related hearing disorders, growing awareness of childhood speech-language delays, and expanding cochlear implant programmes are all driving real demand — while very few colleges actually train BASLP graduates each year. The result is a demand-supply gap the RCI itself describes as needing hundreds of thousands of additional trained professionals.

Fit check

Who should choose Audiology & Speech Therapy

BASLP is a good fit if you...

  • Have strong communication skills and genuine interest in hearing, speech and language science.
  • Want a genuinely accessible healthcare career beyond MBBS, in a field with a severe, well-documented talent shortage.
  • Are drawn specifically to either hearing science (Audiology) or communication disorders (Speech-Language Pathology).

Think twice if you...

  • Aren't willing to research your specific state's limited college options carefully — this is a genuinely niche degree with few programmes.
  • Want to work with movement or daily-living rehabilitation specifically — Occupational Therapy or BPT are the closer match.
  • Expect fresher pay to immediately reflect the genuine severity of the talent shortage — entry-level salaries remain modest despite the strong long-term demand.
Eligibility

Who can apply for BASLP

  • Class 12 pass with Physics, Chemistry and Biology (PCB) or PCM, with a minimum of 50% marks.
  • Minimum age is generally 17 years, with no upper age limit at most institutions.
  • NEET is not required — admission is via each college or state's own entrance exam, or direct merit.

Comparatively affordable, limited seats

Fees & scholarships

BASLP is comparatively affordable, with total course fees commonly ranging from ₹75,000-2,00,000 across the full 4-year programme, though this varies by institute. Specialisation and international opportunity

Higher studies

MSc in Audiology or MSc in Speech-Language Pathology, specialising in one of the two areas, is reported to offer stronger long-term growth than staying generalist at the BASLP level — and generally strengthens both government and private-sector career prospects. Common myths

Myths vs facts

Myth: Audiologist and Speech-Language Pathologist are entirely separate degrees requiring separate admission.

Fact: Both roles come from the same BASLP degree in India — audiology and speech-language pathology are two sub-specialisations within one combined qualification, with career focus developing through clinical interest and later specialisation.

Myth: Since demand is so severe, BASLP freshers must earn very high starting salaries.

Fact: Despite the genuine, severe demand-supply gap, fresher pay remains comparatively modest (₹2.5-4 LPA) — the shortage translates into strong job security and long-term opportunity more than an immediately inflated starting salary.

Myth: This field is basically the same as Occupational Therapy.

Fact: They're genuinely distinct — Audiology and Speech-Language Pathology focus specifically on hearing and communication, while Occupational Therapy focuses on daily-living activities and functional independence more broadly, and comes from a completely separate degree.
