Eligibility

Who can apply for NDA

  • Army Wing: Class 12 pass from a recognised board, any stream — Science, Commerce or Arts.
  • Navy & Air Force Wings: Class 12 pass with Physics and Mathematics specifically required.
  • Unmarried candidates within the age range specified in each official UPSC notification (typically a 3-year window); women are eligible for all wings, including the Naval Academy course.
  • Strict medical and physical fitness standards apply — always verify the latest official notification before applying.
Step-by-step

Your path through NDA

  1. 1

    Class 11–12, correct stream for your wing

    Confirm your preferred wing early — Navy/Air Force need Physics and Maths specifically.

  2. 2

    UPSC written exam

    Mathematics and General Ability Test (GAT), objective type with negative marking.

  3. 3

    5-day SSB interview

    OIR, PPDT, psychology tests, Group Testing Officer tasks, personal interview and conference.

  4. 4

    Medical examination + final merit

    Combined written and SSB marks (out of 1800 total) determine final selection.

  5. 5

    Training (~4 years) + commission

    NDA (3 years) plus a service-specific academy (roughly 1 year), then commissioning as an officer.

What training actually covers

Training & curriculum

PhaseWhat it covers
NDA (3 years)Combined academic training (leading to a BA/BSc/BTech-equivalent degree) alongside military drill, physical training and leadership development, common to all three services
Service-specific academy (~1 year)Indian Military Academy (Army), Indian Naval Academy (Navy), or Air Force Academy (Air Force) — specialised training for the chosen service
CommissioningGraduates are commissioned as a Lieutenant (Army), Sub-Lieutenant (Navy), or Flying Officer (Air Force)

The written exam itself covers Mathematics (300 marks) and a General Ability Test spanning English and General Knowledge (600 marks), for 900 marks total — a genuinely broad academic bar, not a narrow specialist exam.

A genuinely tough, two-gated process

Selection process

Written exam (900 marks)Objective-type Mathematics and General Ability Test, with negative marking; a minimum of 25% is required in each subject to qualify.
SSB Interview (900 marks)A 5-day process: Officer Intelligence Rating and Picture Perception & Description Test on Day 1, then Psychology Tests, Group Testing Officer tasks, a Personal Interview, and a final Conference.
CPSS (Air Force Flying Branch only)Candidates specifically applying for the Flying Branch must also clear the Computerised Pilot Selection System.
Extremely limited seatsOnly a few hundred vacancies per cycle — 394 for NDA 2 2026, split across Army, Navy, Air Force and Naval Academy — against a very large applicant pool, twice yearly.
You're paid to train, not the other way around

Cost & training stipend

Unlike every other career path on this site, NDA involves no tuition fees — training is fully funded, and cadets receive a stipend of ₹56,100 per month throughout the roughly 4-year training period at the NDA and the respective service academy. This is a genuine, significant financial advantage over degree routes that require years of tuition payment before any income begins.

The three services

Wings & roles

Indian ArmyResponsible for land-based military operations and national security; the largest of the three services by personnel.
Indian NavyProtects India's maritime interests and operates naval forces, including the Naval Academy course.
Indian Air ForceProtects Indian airspace and operates military aircraft; the Flying Branch requires additionally clearing CPSS.
Career branchesBeyond combat roles, officers can specialise into engineering, logistics, intelligence, aviation, and administrative branches across all three services.
A structured, merit-based ladder

Career growth

Promotion through the officer ranks — Lieutenant to Captain, Major, Lieutenant Colonel, and beyond — follows a structured, largely merit- and time-based ladder, with genuine opportunities to specialise in engineering, aviation, intelligence or command roles as a career progresses. Officers can also pursue advanced military education at institutions like the Defence Services Staff College, and many senior officers transition into consulting, corporate security leadership, or public service roles after retirement, carrying real, respected credibility from their service record.
