LLB — Becoming a Lawyer in India

10 min read · Bar Council of India-regulated · 5-year route after Class 12, or 3-year after any degree

Law is one of the most well-defined professional careers in Arts — trains you to interpret, apply and argue the law, whether in court, in a corporate legal team, or in policy work. There are two real routes: a 5-year integrated degree straight after Class 12 via CLAT, or a 3-year LLB after finishing any other bachelor's degree first. This guide covers both, honestly, along with what actually happens after you graduate.

Overview

What is LLB?

LLB (Bachelor of Legislative Law) is India's standard law degree, regulated by the Bar Council of India. There are two routes: a 5-year integrated degree — BA LLB, BBA LLB, B.Com LLB or similar — taken directly after Class 12, combining a general bachelor's degree with legal training; and a 3-year LLB taken after completing any bachelor's degree first. Both lead to the same professional qualification and eligibility to practise after clearing the All India Bar Examination (AIBE).

The 5-year integrated route is offered at the National Law Universities (NLUs) — like NLSIU Bengaluru, NALSAR Hyderabad and NLU Delhi — along with hundreds of other law colleges nationwide, admitted primarily through CLAT. The 3-year route is offered widely at university law faculties, including Delhi University's Faculty of Law and many state universities.

Fit check

Who should choose law

Law is a good fit if you...

  • Enjoy precise reading, constructing arguments, and finding the exact meaning of a written rule.
  • Are comfortable with a demanding, competitive path — top NLUs and good firm placements both require sustained effort.
  • Are drawn to litigation, corporate law, policy work or judicial services specifically — not just choosing law as a "safe" professional label.

Think twice if you...

  • Expect a guaranteed high income right after graduation — early litigation income especially can be modest and slow to build.
  • Dislike heavy reading of dense, technical text — statutes, case law and judgments are the bulk of the coursework.
  • Aren't prepared for the sustained CLAT preparation needed for a top NLU seat — competition is intense.
Eligibility

Who can apply for LLB

  • 5-year integrated route: Class 12 pass (10+2) from any stream, typically 45-50% aggregate minimum, with an upper age limit at some NLUs (varies by institute).
  • 3-year route: Any bachelor's degree (BA, B.Com, B.Sc, etc.) with minimum required marks, usually 45-50%, varying by university.
  • Open to students from any Class 12 or graduation stream — no specific prior subject is compulsory for either route.

The main entrance route

CLAT — exam details

Conducting authorityConsortium of National Law Universities, for admission to NLUs and many affiliated law colleges nationwide.
Who takes itClass 12 students (any stream) for the 5-year integrated UG programme, and law graduates for the PG (LLM) programme.
Exam sectionsEnglish Language, Current Affairs including General Knowledge, Legal Reasoning, Logical Reasoning, and Quantitative Techniques.
Other examsAILET (for NLU Delhi specifically), LSAT-India, and several state-level and private-university law entrance tests exist as alternatives.
After qualifyingCounselling rounds based on rank, seat allotment across participating NLUs and colleges, and document verification.
Wide range by institute type

Fees & scholarships

Fees vary substantially by institute. National Law Universities typically charge ₹2-3.5 lakh per year, while Delhi University's Faculty of Law and many state university law departments are far more affordable, often ₹10,000-40,000 per year. Private law colleges can range widely, from moderate to over ₹2 lakh per year. Merit-based and need-based scholarships are available at most NLUs and state institutions. Always confirm current fees directly on the official institute website.

Beyond the degree

Higher studies

An LLM (Master of Laws) — at NLUs, Delhi University or abroad — lets graduates specialise in areas like corporate law, intellectual property, constitutional law or international law, and is often preferred for academic or highly specialised corporate roles. Some graduates instead prepare for Judicial Services exams to become a judge, while others pursue further certifications in arbitration, taxation or compliance to strengthen a specific practice area.

Honest take

Advantages and disadvantages

Advantages

  • A clearly defined, well-regulated professional qualification with strong long-term earning potential.
  • Genuinely diverse — litigation, corporate law, judiciary, policy and academia are all real paths from the same degree.
  • Top NLU graduates have strong campus placement outcomes into major corporate law firms.

Disadvantages

  • Very wide, unpredictable income range — early litigation income especially can be modest for several years.
  • CLAT and top NLU admission are highly competitive, requiring serious dedicated preparation.
  • Outcomes vary sharply by college tier — placement quality at a top NLU versus a lesser-known law college differs significantly.
Choosing your route

5-Year Integrated LLB vs 3-Year LLB

Factor5-Year Integrated (after 12th)3-Year LLB (after a degree)
When you startDirectly after Class 12After completing any bachelor's degree
Entrance examCLAT / AILET / LSAT-IndiaUniversity-specific entrance or merit
Total time to LLB5 years3 years, but only after a 3-4 year degree first
Best forStudents sure about law right after schoolStudents who decide on law later, or want another degree first

Both lead to the same professional qualification once you clear the All India Bar Examination. The integrated route is faster and dominates top NLU placements; the 3-year route suits students who want a different first degree, like Political Science or Commerce, before specialising in law.

Common myths

Myths vs facts

Myth: Only Arts students can study law.

Fact: Law is open to students from any Class 12 stream — Commerce and Science-background students are common at top NLUs, and a Commerce background can even help with corporate law electives.

Myth: Every lawyer earns a very high salary.

Fact: Income varies enormously — top-tier corporate law firm hires earn strongly, but many litigators build income gradually over years, especially early in independent practice.

Myth: A law degree only leads to court practice.

Fact: Graduates also work in corporate law, in-house legal teams, compliance, judicial services, policy drafting and academia — litigation is one visible path among several.
