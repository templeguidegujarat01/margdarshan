The basics

What is CUET-UG, and why does it exist?

CUET-UG (Common University Entrance Test — Undergraduate) is a national-level entrance exam conducted by the National Testing Agency (NTA) for admission to undergraduate programmes at participating universities across India. Before CUET, Class 12 board marks alone were often used to shortlist candidates for university admission, which made comparison difficult across different state boards with different marking patterns. CUET gives every participating university a common, standardised score to compare candidates on instead.

CUET-UG is used by 200+ universities, including 48 Central Universities (such as Delhi University, Banaras Hindu University, Jawaharlal Nehru University and Jamia Millia Islamia) plus a number of state, deemed and private universities that opt in — for a wide range of undergraduate Science, Commerce, Arts and other degree programmes.

Is this for you?

Who needs to take CUET-UG?

Any student aiming for undergraduate admission at a university that uses CUET-UG for its selection process — this spans Science, Commerce and Arts programmes, not just one stream.

You should consider it if:

You're finishing Class 12 and want to apply to Delhi University, BHU, JNU, or another CUET-participating university — including for Science degrees like Biotechnology, Microbiology, Forensic Science, Genetics or Environmental Science where NEET is not required.

You probably don't need it if:

You're specifically targeting IITs/NITs (which use JEE Main/Advanced instead) or MBBS/BDS/AYUSH/Nursing seats (which use NEET-UG instead) — CUET-UG is a separate, general-admission exam from both.

Basic requirements

Eligibility, at a stable level

The standard requirement is having passed or currently appearing for Class 12 (or an equivalent qualification) from a recognised board, with minimum marks that vary slightly by category (commonly around 45% for general category and 40% for SC/ST/OBC/PwBD, though this is set fresh each cycle). There's no upper age limit. Recent CUET-UG cycles have also allowed candidates to choose exam subjects regardless of what they studied in Class 12, though each participating university can still set its own course-specific eligibility on top of the CUET score.

Exact details like minimum qualifying marks, the current list of offered subjects, and category-wise relaxations are set fresh in each year's official information bulletin and can change. Always verify current-year eligibility on the official CUET website before assuming you qualify.

How the exam works

Exam pattern — the stable structure

CUET-UG is a computer-based test (CBT) with multiple-choice questions, generally structured across a Language section, Domain-Specific Subject papers (matched to the course you want), and a General Test — candidates typically choose a set number of subjects relevant to their target course. It's held over multiple days/shifts each year at exam centres across India, rather than on a single day like NEET-UG.

Specific details such as the exact number of subjects allowed, marking scheme and exam duration per paper are published fresh in each year's official notification. Always confirm the current pattern via the official CUET notification rather than assuming it matches a previous year — the subject list and structure have changed across recent cycles.

Once you're done

What happens after the exam?

After results are declared, admission happens separately at each participating university, not through one common counselling body the way NEET-UG has MCC. Each university publishes its own merit list or conducts its own counselling round based on your CUET-UG score, and course-specific or category-specific cutoffs vary by university and even by college within a university (such as Delhi University's constituent colleges). Your score, category, and the specific university/course you're applying to together determine your options — qualifying CUET-UG alone does not guarantee a seat at any particular university.

Getting ready

How students usually prepare

CUET-UG preparation is generally lighter than JEE or NEET, since it's built closely around the NCERT Class 12 syllabus for whichever subjects you choose, rather than requiring an entirely separate competitive syllabus. Most students prepare through focused NCERT revision, previous years' question papers, and short-term coaching or self-study in the final months before the exam — long-term multi-year coaching is far less common for CUET-UG than for JEE/NEET.
