Overview

What is Mechanical Engineering?

Mechanical Engineering is a 4-year engineering degree focused on designing, building, testing and maintaining machines, engines, tools and mechanical systems — everything from a car's engine to a factory's production line to a power plant's turbines. Its foundation rests on four pillars: engineering mechanics, thermodynamics, fluid mechanics and machine design.

The field has genuinely modernised in the last decade. Mechanical engineers today increasingly work alongside software and electronics teams on Industry 4.0, automation, electric vehicles and robotics — the core physics hasn't changed, but the tools and the collaborations around it have.

Fit check

Who should choose Mechanical Engineering

Mechanical Engineering is a good fit if you...

  • Genuinely enjoy understanding how physical machines and systems work, not just how software behaves.
  • Want a realistic shot at government/PSU stability through GATE, alongside a private-sector option.
  • Are drawn to automobiles, manufacturing, energy, aerospace or defence rather than pure software.

Think twice if you...

  • Expect private-sector fresher salaries similar to CSE — the honest starting range is meaningfully lower at most colleges.
  • Want a fully remote-friendly career — many core mechanical roles involve plant, site or shop-floor presence.
  • Aren't willing to add skills like CAD/simulation software, automation or GATE prep — a plain degree alone increasingly limits your options.
Eligibility

Who can apply for Mechanical Engineering

  • Class 12 pass with Physics, Chemistry and Mathematics (PCM) from a recognised board.
  • For Indian Institutes of Technology (IITs) and National Institutes of Technology (NITs) via Joint Entrance Examination (JEE), a minimum of 75% aggregate in Class 12 (65% for SC/ST) is required for counselling eligibility.
  • Diploma holders in Mechanical Engineering can join directly in the second year through most states' lateral entry route, skipping JEE and the first year entirely.

Fees & scholarships

Government-funded institutes remain the most economical route: total fees for the full 4-year Mechanical Engineering programme at IITs and NITs commonly fall in the ₹2-8 lakh range, with fee waivers available based on family income and category. Private colleges vary widely — total costs of roughly ₹5-18 lakh are typical, with some premium private institutes going higher. Merit-based scholarships and category-based fee concessions are available at most institutes; always confirm current fees directly on your target college's official admission page.

Private vs PSU — the real trade-off

Beyond the degree

Higher studies

An M.Tech via GATE is the standard route into IITs/NITs for research roles, teaching, or a stronger PSU application. Others pursue an MS abroad for advanced design or R&D roles, an Master of Business Administration (MBA) for a move into operations or management, or focused certifications in areas like Robotics, Mechatronics, EV Technology or simulation software (ANSYS, CATIA) — increasingly the fastest way to stand out for private-sector roles.

Honest take

Advantages and disadvantages

Advantages

  • The widest PSU and government hiring footprint of any engineering branch, through GATE.
  • Genuinely resilient to tech hiring slowdowns — core industries like refineries and defence plants don't follow software funding cycles.
  • Growing new demand from EVs, automation and Industry 4.0, alongside evergreen core-industry roles.

Disadvantages

  • Private-sector fresher salaries are meaningfully lower than CSE or Electronics and Communication Engineering (ECE) at most colleges.
  • Standing out for a high private-sector package increasingly requires added skills — automation, simulation software, GATE prep — not just the degree.
  • Many core roles involve plant, site or shop-floor presence rather than remote-friendly desk work.

Common myths

Myths vs facts

Myth: Mechanical Engineering is a "dying" branch.

Fact: Core industries — manufacturing, defence, energy, EVs — continue hiring steadily. What's changed is that added skills like automation, data and simulation software now matter more for a strong private-sector salary, not that demand has disappeared.

Myth: Mechanical engineers earn far less than CSE engineers across the board.

Fact: Private freshers usually do earn less, but Mechanical has the widest PSU/GATE presence of any branch — those government roles offer strong pay plus stability that many CSE graduates don't have.

Myth: A plain mechanical degree limits you to old-fashioned factory work.

Fact: Mechanical engineers today work on EVs, robotics, automation and renewable energy — the field has genuinely modernised alongside software and electronics teams.
