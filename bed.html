B.Ed — Becoming a School Teacher in India

9 min read · NCTE-regulated teacher training degree · After any bachelor's degree, or 4-year integrated after 12th

B.Ed (Bachelor of Education) trains graduates to teach at the school level — covering pedagogy, child psychology, curriculum design and classroom management. It's the standard qualifying route into school teaching in India, but on its own it's just the first step: to teach in a government school, you also need to clear CTET or your state's TET. Overview

What is B.Ed?

B.Ed (Bachelor of Education) is a professional teacher-training degree regulated by the National Council for Teacher Education (NCTE), designed to prepare graduates to teach at the secondary and senior secondary school level. It covers educational psychology, pedagogy (how to actually teach a subject effectively), curriculum planning, classroom management, and includes supervised teaching practice in real schools.

The standard route is a 2-year B.Ed after completing any bachelor's degree. Under NEP 2020, a 4-year Integrated B.A B.Ed / B.Sc B.Ed is also increasingly offered, letting students combine a regular degree with teacher training in one continuous programme straight after Class 12, at institutions like Regional Institutes of Education (RIEs) and several state universities.

Fit check

Who should choose B.Ed

B.Ed is a good fit if you...

  • Genuinely enjoy explaining things to others and want a stable, meaningful career shaping young minds.
  • Already have (or plan to complete) a bachelor's degree in the subject you want to teach.
  • Are prepared for the additional CTET/TET exam step needed to teach in government schools.

Think twice if you...

  • See teaching as a fallback option rather than a genuine interest — classroom management is demanding, daily work.
  • Expect high pay quickly — government teaching salaries are stable but not typically the highest-paying Arts route.
  • Aren't willing to prepare for CTET/TET on top of the degree — many capable B.Ed graduates struggle here without dedicated prep.
Eligibility

Who can apply for B.Ed

  • 2-year B.Ed: A bachelor's degree (BA, B.Com, B.Sc, etc.) in any discipline, typically with 50% aggregate for General category and relaxed criteria for reserved categories, varying by state/university.
  • 4-year Integrated B.A/B.Sc B.Ed: Class 12 pass (10+2) from any stream, admitted through entrance exams like the National Common Entrance Test (NCET) for select institutes.
  • Most states also conduct their own B.Ed entrance exams for admission to state-affiliated colleges.

Affordable at government colleges

Fees & scholarships

B.Ed fees at government and state university colleges are typically in the range of ₹20,000-60,000 per year, while private B.Ed colleges can charge considerably more, often ₹50,000-1.5 lakh per year depending on the state and institute. Merit-based, SC/ST and income-linked scholarships are available at most government institutions. Always confirm current fees directly on your target college's official admission page.

Honest take

Advantages and disadvantages

Advantages

  • Clear, well-defined path into a stable, respected profession with structured government pay scales.
  • Reasonable working hours and school-term schedules compared to many other careers.
  • Genuine, meaningful long-term impact through shaping students' learning and development.

Disadvantages

  • B.Ed alone doesn't guarantee a government teaching job — CTET/TET and a separate recruitment exam are also required.
  • Government teacher recruitment cycles can be infrequent, with long gaps between vacancy announcements in some states.
  • Private school pay and job security vary considerably, with less standardisation than government posts.

Common myths

Myths vs facts

Myth: A B.Ed degree alone is enough to get a government teaching job.

Fact: You also need to clear CTET or your state TET, and then a separate school/state recruitment exam — B.Ed makes you eligible to attempt these, not automatically employed.

Myth: Teaching is an easy, low-effort career.

Fact: Effective teaching requires ongoing lesson planning, classroom management, assessment and genuine subject mastery — it's demanding, sustained daily work.

Myth: Any graduate can teach any subject with a B.Ed.

Fact: The subject you're qualified to teach is generally tied to your undergraduate subject specialisation — a B.Ed doesn't substitute for subject-matter expertise.
