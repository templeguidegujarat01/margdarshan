Overview

What is CDS?

CDS (Combined Defence Services Examination) is conducted twice a year by the Union Public Service Commission (UPSC) to select graduates for officer training at four institutions: the Indian Military Academy (IMA) for the Army, Indian Naval Academy (INA) for the Navy, Air Force Academy (AFA), and the Officers Training Academy (OTA) for Short Service Commission officers in the Army.

Unlike NDA, which starts right after Class 12 and combines academic study with military training over 3 years, CDS is for candidates who've already completed a bachelor's degree — making it a faster, more direct route into officer ranks for graduates who decide on a defence career later, or who didn't pursue NDA at 12th grade.

Fit check

Who should pursue CDS

CDS is a good fit if you...

  • Decided on a defence career during or after your bachelor's degree, rather than right after Class 12.
  • Have genuine physical fitness, discipline and leadership qualities the SSB is specifically designed to assess.
  • Want a faster route to a commissioned officer role than starting over with NDA-level training.

Think twice if you...

  • Are still in Class 12 and sure about defence — NDA may suit you better as the earlier entry point.
  • Are not prepared for the demanding 5-day SSB assessment, which tests far more than academic knowledge.
  • Aren't comfortable with the discipline, transfers and structured lifestyle that come with military service.
Eligibility

Who can apply for CDS

  • IMA (Army) & OTA: A bachelor's degree in any discipline from a recognised university — open to Arts, Commerce and Science graduates alike.
  • AFA (Air Force) & INA (Navy): Typically require an engineering degree or a bachelor's degree with Physics and Mathematics at the Class 12 level — check the specific entry's current requirement.
  • Age limits vary by entry, typically 19-25 years, with women eligible for the OTA entry and expanding eligibility in other entries — always verify against the latest official notification.

Exam pattern & SSB

Written exam (IMA/INA/AFA)Three papers — English, General Knowledge and Elementary Mathematics — each objective-type.
Written exam (OTA)Two papers — English and General Knowledge — since Mathematics isn't required for this entry.
SSB InterviewA rigorous 5-day process covering psychological tests, group tasks, group discussions, an interview and physical tasks, assessing "Officer-Like Qualities" beyond academics.

Honest take

Advantages and disadvantages

Advantages

  • Faster route into an officer role for graduates than starting NDA-level training from scratch.
  • IMA/OTA entries are genuinely open to any graduation discipline, including Arts.
  • Strong job security, respect and substantial non-cash benefits alongside base pay.

Disadvantages

  • The SSB is a genuinely demanding, high-attrition stage — many who clear the written exam don't clear the SSB.
  • Frequent postings and transfers are part of military life, offering less location control than civilian careers.
  • Physical and psychological demands of the role are significant and ongoing, not just during selection.

Common myths

Myths vs facts

Myth: Only Science/Engineering graduates can apply for CDS.

Fact: The IMA (Army) and OTA entries are open to graduates of any discipline, including Arts — only the AFA (Air Force) and INA (Navy) entries have specific stream requirements.

Myth: Clearing the written exam means you've secured a spot.

Fact: The SSB interview afterward is often the more decisive, challenging stage — many candidates who clear the written exam don't clear the SSB.

Myth: Women can't apply for CDS.

Fact: Women can apply through the OTA entry for a Short Service Commission, and eligibility has expanded in recent years for other entries too — always check the latest notification.
