Diploma in Interior Design

8 min read · State board/institute-recognised diploma · Open after Class 10 or 12

Diploma in Interior Design trains students to plan and design residential, commercial and retail interiors — layouts, materials, furniture, lighting and colour schemes — combining design theory with heavy studio work and software skills like AutoCAD and SketchUp. It's a practical, portfolio-driven route into a creative field, with genuine freelance and self-employment potential built in. This guide covers exactly what the diploma involves and where it leads.

Overview

What is Diploma in Interior Design?

Diploma in Interior Design trains students to plan and design the interiors of residential, commercial and institutional spaces — deciding layouts, choosing materials, finishes, furniture, lighting and colour schemes, while keeping structural, budget and client needs in mind. It sits in the same "Architecture & Design" diploma family as Diploma in Architecture, but focuses specifically on interior spaces rather than the building structure itself.

The course is deliberately practical. Alongside classroom subjects, students spend substantial time on studio work, drawing, model-making and computer-aided design, building a portfolio of projects along the way — which matters just as much as the certificate itself when applying for jobs or freelance work in this field. It's offered at government polytechnics, private design institutes and a few specialised interior design schools across India.

Fit check

Who should choose this Diploma

This Diploma is a good fit if you...

  • Enjoy visual thinking, spatial planning and working with colours, materials and layouts.
  • Want a practical, faster route into a creative field without waiting through a full degree first.
  • Are interested in eventually freelancing or running your own design practice.

Think twice if you...

  • Specifically want to become a licensed Architect — that needs a B.Arch and Council of Architecture registration, not this diploma.
  • Are uncomfortable with income uncertainty, especially if freelancing is your main goal.
  • Strongly prefer a stable, salaried path from day one over a portfolio-driven, reputation-based one.
Eligibility

Who can apply

  • Class 10 pass, typically requiring around 35-50% aggregate, varying by state and institute — no specific stream is usually mandatory, though comfort with drawing and Maths helps.
  • A shorter 1-2 year diploma option exists at several institutes for Class 12 pass students, from any stream (Science, Commerce or Arts).
  • Exact eligibility criteria, minimum marks and course duration vary meaningfully by institute — there's no single uniform national format, so always confirm with your target institute's current admission notification.

Fees & scholarships

Government polytechnic fees for the diploma are generally the most affordable option, typically in the range of ₹15,000-60,000 for the full course. Private design institutes charge considerably more, often ₹60,000-2 lakh per year, sometimes higher at institutes with strong industry reputations or metro-city locations. Budget additionally for drafting/model-making materials, a laptop capable of running design software, and software licenses (though many institutes provide lab access or student-edition licenses). Merit-based and income-linked scholarships are available at most government institutions. Always confirm current fees directly with your target institute's admission office.

Beyond the diploma

Higher studies & degree progression

A Bachelor of Design (B.Des) in Interior Design is the natural next step for diploma holders who want deeper design theory, larger studio projects and stronger placement access. Some institutes offer lateral entry into the second year for diploma holders, though this isn't as uniformly standardised as engineering lateral entry — check each institute's specific policy. A B.Arch is a different, longer, legally regulated professional degree for those who specifically want to become licensed Architects rather than interior designers; it is not a simple continuation of this diploma. Short certifications in 3D rendering, sustainable/green design, lighting design or advanced software (Revit, V-Ray) can also meaningfully strengthen a diploma-holder's profile without a full second degree.

Honest take

Advantages and disadvantages

Advantages

  • Genuinely strong self-employment and freelance potential, since the title isn't legally restricted.
  • Hands-on, portfolio-driven course that employers and clients value as much as the certificate itself.
  • Faster and cheaper entry into the design industry than a full B.Des or B.Arch.

Disadvantages

  • No standardised national syllabus or duration — quality and depth vary significantly by institute.
  • Freelance income can be inconsistent early on and depends heavily on networking and referrals.
  • Larger commercial or high-end residential projects often favour B.Des/B.Arch-qualified designers with bigger studios.

Choose Interior Design if you're drawn to spaces, materials and finishes with genuine freelance flexibility; choose Architecture if your goal is eventually becoming a licensed Architect who can sign off on building structures.

Common myths

Myths vs facts

Myth: This diploma makes you a licensed Architect.

Fact: Architect is a legally protected title requiring a B.Arch degree and Council of Architecture registration. This diploma qualifies you to design interiors, not to sign off on structural building changes.

Myth: Interior design is only for students who are naturally good at drawing.

Fact: Freehand sketching helps, but the field leans just as heavily on spatial planning, material knowledge, budgeting and software skills — many designers build drawing skill during the course itself.

Myth: You need a government license to work as an interior designer in India.

Fact: Interior design isn't a government-licensed profession in India. Professional credibility comes from your qualification, portfolio and reputation — voluntary bodies like IIID support this but aren't legally mandatory.
