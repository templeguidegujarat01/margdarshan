Overview

What is Robotics Engineering?

Robotics Engineering is a genuinely interdisciplinary field, combining mechanical design, electronics and embedded systems, and programming/AI to build and control machines that sense, decide and act — industrial robotic arms, autonomous vehicles, drones, humanoid robots and medical robotics all fall under it.

The growth numbers here are real, not just marketing: India recorded a 59% surge in industrial robot installations between 2022 and 2023 and is now among the world's top 10 markets for annual robot installations, driven by Make in India, Industry 4.0 adoption and the Production Linked Incentive scheme. What's less commonly explained is that this growth doesn't automatically require a standalone "Robotics" degree — a large share of the people building this industry studied a core branch and specialised.

Fit check

Who should choose Robotics Engineering

Robotics is a good fit if you...

  • Genuinely enjoy working across hardware and software together, rather than deep expertise in just one.
  • Want to be part of India's genuinely growing automation and Industry 4.0 wave.
  • Are willing to build a real project portfolio (ROS projects, robotics competitions, internships) — this matters more here than in most branches.

Think twice if you...

  • Want a long-established, widely recognised degree name with a large, mature alumni and recruiter network.
  • Prefer deep specialisation in a single discipline over breadth across mechanical, electronics and software.
  • Expect the degree alone, without a project portfolio, to guarantee a strong outcome — in this field, skills matter more than the degree name.
Eligibility

Who can apply for Robotics Engineering

  • Class 12 pass with Physics, Chemistry and Mathematics (PCM) from a recognised board.
  • Minimum aggregate typically 50-60% at most colleges offering a dedicated Robotics programme — generally more flexible than IIT/NIT cutoffs for core branches.
  • Alternatively, standard PCM eligibility for Mechanical, Electronics or Computer Science if you choose the specialise-later route instead.

Wide range by institute type

Fees & scholarships

Government-funded institutes offering Robotics or Mechatronics-adjacent programmes remain the most economical route: total fees for the full 4-year programme commonly fall in the ₹2-8 lakh range, with fee waivers available based on family income and category. Private colleges vary considerably — total costs of roughly ₹6-20 lakh are typical, since many Robotics-specific programmes sit at newer, often private institutions. Merit-based scholarships are available at some institutes; always confirm current fees directly on your target college's official admission page.

Beyond the degree

Higher studies

An M.Tech in Robotics or Mechatronics via GATE is the standard route into IITs/NITs for research and specialised industrial roles. An MS abroad — at globally recognised robotics programmes like Carnegie Mellon or Georgia Tech — is common for students targeting advanced research or global robotics companies. Focused certifications in ROS, PLC/SCADA or AI/ML for robotics can meaningfully speed up entry into better-paid specialised roles without a full postgraduate degree.

Honest take

Advantages and disadvantages

Advantages

  • Riding a genuine, measurable growth wave — a real 59% surge in industrial robot installations, not just hype.
  • Interdisciplinary skill set (mechanical, electronics, software) transfers broadly across industries.
  • Specialisation in AI/ML-robotics is reported among the field's highest experienced-professional salary bands.

Disadvantages

  • A dedicated Robotics UG degree is still relatively new and less standardised in India than Mechanical, ECE or CSE.
  • Entry salary depends heavily on your own project portfolio and skills (ROS, computer vision, PLC), more than in most branches.
  • "Robotics Engineer" job titles and actual responsibilities vary widely between companies — research the real role, not just the title.

Common myths

Myths vs facts

Myth: You need a dedicated "Robotics Engineering" degree to become a robotics engineer.

Fact: Most working robotics engineers in India actually studied Mechanical, Electronics or Computer Science and specialised in robotics through electives, projects and certifications — the job comes from skills, not just the degree name.

Myth: Robotics is a tiny, futuristic niche with few real jobs today.

Fact: India recorded a genuine 59% surge in industrial robot installations between 2022 and 2023 and is now among the world's top 10 markets for robot installations — this is an active, growing hiring area, not just a research curiosity.

Myth: Any Robotics degree automatically leads to the ₹20-25 LPA salaries reported for experienced professionals.

Fact: Those figures apply to experienced professionals with genuine AI/ML specialisation; a fresher's realistic starting point is meaningfully lower and depends heavily on your actual project portfolio.
