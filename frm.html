FRM — Financial Risk Manager

11 min read · Regulated by GARP (USA) · No eligibility prerequisites to sit the exam

FRM is the most specialised of the major finance credentials — narrowly focused on identifying, measuring and managing financial risk. Issued by the Global Association of Risk Professionals (GARP) and held by 97,000+ professionals across 190+ countries, it's also the most open: unlike CFA or CPA, there's no degree or age requirement to even register.

Overview

What is FRM?

Financial Risk Manager (FRM) is a certification issued by the Global Association of Risk Professionals (GARP), focused entirely on the tools, models and frameworks used to measure and manage financial risk — market risk, credit risk, operational risk, and liquidity risk. Where CFA covers the broad craft of investing, FRM goes deep on one specific function: understanding exactly how much risk an institution is carrying, and what to do about it.

Independent benchmarking by UK ENIC has assessed the FRM's difficulty as comparable to a Master's degree. It's the credential banks, asset managers, regulators and consulting firms look for when hiring into VaR modelling, stress testing, Basel-compliance and model validation roles — work that has only grown more central as regulators like the RBI have tightened requirements on Indian financial institutions.

Fit check

Who should choose FRM — and who should think twice

FRM is a good fit if you...

  • Are comfortable with statistics, probability and quantitative reasoning, and want a narrowly-specialised finance credential.
  • Want the lowest-barrier entry point among major global finance credentials — no degree needed to start.
  • Are specifically drawn to risk analytics, model validation, or regulatory capital work rather than general investing.

Think twice if you...

  • Dislike heavily quantitative material — FRM leans more mathematical than CA, CS or ACCA.
  • Want a broad investment-management career — CFA covers more ground for portfolio and equity-research roles.
  • Already hold CA, CFA or CPA and expect exemptions — GARP grants none, regardless of prior qualification.
The most open eligibility of any course on this site

Who can register

GARP imposes no formal educational prerequisites to register for FRM Part I — no minimum degree, no age requirement, no prior work experience. You can technically begin during your undergraduate years, or even earlier. In practice, most candidates do hold at least a bachelor's degree by the time they attempt the exam, simply because the material assumes comfort with probability, statistics, calculus and financial concepts — but this is a matter of practical preparation, not a GARP rule.

Important distinction: the exam has no prerequisites, but the full FRM certification (using the "FRM" designation after your name) requires 2 years of relevant work experience, covered in detail further down this page.

Passing criteria

Like CFA, GARP does not publish a fixed pass percentage. The passing score for each exam is determined by the FRM exam committee after each sitting, and your result is reported as a simple pass or fail, along with a quartile showing how your performance compared to other candidates in each topic area (not a raw score).

Important: GARP grants no exemptions to any part of the FRM exam for any prior qualification — not for CA, CFA, CPA, or any other credential. Every candidate passes both parts independently, and Part II must be passed by December 31 of the 4th year after passing Part I, or your Part I result expires and you must re-sit it.
Beyond the exams

Work experience & using the FRM designation

Passing both exam parts alone doesn't let you use the FRM designation. GARP requires 2 years of full-time, relevant professional work experience in financial risk management or a closely related field — roles like risk analysis, model validation, trading, portfolio management, treasury, audit or consulting all qualify.

  • Experience can be accrued before, during, or after you sit the exams — many candidates already have the two years by the time they pass Part II.
  • You have 10 years after passing Part II to submit and get this experience verified.
  • Internships, part-time jobs and student work do not count toward the requirement.

Set expectations correctly

Difficulty & approximate pass rates

Most candidates report studying roughly 250 hours per part. Recent pass rates have run approximately 45–58% for Part I and 50–63% for Part II (Part II has historically had a somewhat higher pass rate, since only candidates who've already cleared Part I attempt it) — these move with each sitting, so treat them as a general sense of difficulty rather than a fixed target. Most candidates complete both parts within 1–2 years.

Looking ahead

Future scope & the impact of AI

Risk management is one of the parts of finance least likely to shrink from automation — if anything, AI and algorithmic trading create new categories of risk (model risk, algorithmic bias, data risk) that need human oversight. Routine reporting is increasingly automated, but judging whether a risk model's assumptions are actually sound, and deciding how an institution should respond to a stress scenario, remains squarely human work.

Growing regulatory pressure from the RBI and SEBI, along with the rapid growth of India's banking, NBFC and fintech risk functions, has kept demand for FRM-certified professionals rising rather than falling. FRMs who add Python and machine-learning skills to their core risk toolkit are especially well positioned, since quantitative risk modelling increasingly overlaps with data science.

Honest take

Advantages and disadvantages

Advantages

  • No formal eligibility barrier — the most accessible entry point among major global finance credentials.
  • Just 2 exams, both completable within 1–2 years for most candidates.
  • Strong, growing demand in India driven by tightening banking and NBFC regulation.

Disadvantages

  • Highly specialised — narrower career scope than CFA or a general finance degree if you later change direction.
  • Heavily quantitative — a genuine barrier if statistics and calculus aren't your strength.
  • No campus placement system like an MBA — landing your first risk role still depends on your own networking and resume work.

Common myths

Myths vs facts

Myth: You need a finance degree to even register for FRM.

Fact: GARP has no formal educational prerequisites at all for FRM Part I — anyone can register.

Myth: Passing both FRM exams automatically makes you "FRM Certified."

Fact: You also need 2 years of verified relevant work experience before you can use the FRM designation.

Myth: A CFA charterholder gets some FRM papers waived.

Fact: GARP grants zero exemptions to anyone, regardless of any other qualification held.

Practical advice

Expert tips for FRM candidates

  • Master Part I's quantitative foundations properly — Part II assumes you can apply them fluently, and re-learning them later costs real time.
  • Don't underestimate Foundations of Risk Management — the governance and case-study questions here trip up candidates who over-focus on the quantitative sections.
  • Sit Part II soon after passing Part I — a long gap means re-learning material rather than building on it.
  • If you're already in a risk-adjacent role (audit, compliance, trading support), start documenting your responsibilities now — it may already count toward the 2-year experience requirement.
