Admission to engineering, pharmacy, medical and diploma courses in India often depends on your state. Each state runs its own entrance test or counselling, keeps a share of seats for local candidates, and operates its own polytechnic board and scholarship portal. This hub puts those rules side by side for ten major states so that you can see which exam to prepare for, how quota and domicile affect your chances, and where to apply for financial help.

Overview

How state admissions work

Three layers decide where you can study: the entrance test or merit list (state CET, JEE Main or NEET-UG), the counselling process run by a state authority, and the seat matrix that splits seats into state quota, all-India or other-state quota, category reservation and management quota. Your Class 12 school state, domicile certificate and category certificate often matter as much as your score.

Entrance or merit

State CETs (GUJCET, MHT-CET, KCET, WBJEE, EAPCET), JEE Main-based counselling, or Class 12 merit such as TNEA.

Quota & domicile

Local candidates get priority for most state-quota seats, with separate shares for other states and categories.

Counselling

Rounds of registration, choice filling and seat allotment run by a state committee or board.

Smart preparation

Application tips for state admissions

1Documents

Get certificates early

Domicile, caste or category, income and non-creamy-layer certificates can take weeks; apply before the counselling window opens.

2Documents

Keep school proofs ready

Many state quotas depend on where you studied Class 10 and 12; keep mark sheets and leaving certificates handy.

3Planning

Stack your options

Combine your home-state CET with JEE Main or NEET so you have both state-quota and all-India seats in play.

4Planning

Read the brochure

The information brochure lists eligibility, seat matrix, fees and dates; it is the only binding source.

5Counselling

Fill choices carefully

Order your choices by preference, not by chance; the allotment algorithm follows your list exactly.

6Money

Apply for scholarships in parallel

Scholarship windows run alongside admission; apply as soon as your college is confirmed.

Good to know

Frequently asked questions

A state Common Entrance Test (CET) such as GUJCET, MHT-CET or KCET is used for admission to engineering, pharmacy and other courses in that state's colleges. Medical (MBBS, BDS, AYUSH) admission in every state uses NEET-UG. Some states, including Delhi, Uttar Pradesh and Bihar, use JEE Main scores for engineering counselling instead of a separate state test.
In government and aided colleges, most seats are reserved for candidates from that state, decided by domicile, where you passed Class 12 or both. The rest are open to candidates from other states or the all-India pool. Every state publishes its own rules, so read the admission brochure.
Yes, you can appear for several state tests, but your chances for state-quota seats depend on each state's eligibility rules. Many students combine their home-state CET with JEE Main or NEET for wider options.
Each state has a technical education board or directorate that runs polytechnic diploma admission, usually after Class 10, and a state technical board or university that sets syllabus and exams. Diploma holders can often move into the second year of B.Tech through lateral entry.
Each state runs its own scholarship portal for post-matric and professional courses, and central schemes are available on the National Scholarship Portal. Eligibility depends on income, category and marks, and the application window is announced each year.

*Exam names, conducting bodies and quota rules change between admission cycles. Always confirm details on the official state portal and in the current information brochure.
