Company Secretary (CS)

8 min read · Regulated by ICSI · Open after Class 12, any stream

A Company Secretary is the corporate governance and compliance expert every company needs — the professional who keeps a company's board decisions, filings and legal obligations in order. Like CA, it's earned through ICSI's own exams, not a university degree.

Overview

What is a Company Secretary?

A Company Secretary is a certified professional, governed by the Institute of Company Secretaries of India (ICSI) under the Ministry of Corporate Affairs, who ensures a company follows corporate law, SEBI regulations and good governance practice. Every listed company and many large private companies are legally required to appoint one.

CS professionals draft board resolutions, manage annual filings, advise on mergers and restructuring, and act as the link between a company's board, its shareholders and regulators. Many later move into legal, compliance-head or company-secretary-cum-legal-head roles.

Eligibility

Who should choose CS, and when to start

CS suits students who enjoy law, structured writing and governance more than pure numbers — you don't need a Commerce background to succeed.

After Class 10

  • You can complete Class 12 in any stream — Commerce, Science or Arts, all lead to CS equally well.
  • Strong English and reasoning skills help more than Maths at this stage.

After Class 12

  • Register for and clear CSEET (CS Executive Entrance Test) — open to all streams, no minimum percentage.
  • Graduates and postgraduates (except Fine Arts) can skip CSEET and register directly for CS Executive.
Exam structure

Paper-wise syllabus & pass criteria

CS has three levels — CSEET, Executive and Professional. The pass rule is consistent throughout: 40% per paper · 50% aggregate in a single attempt. ICSI updates syllabi periodically, so always cross-check paper names on icsi.edu before registering.

Level 1 — CSEET (entrance test)

400 marks · 4 papers
PaperSubjectFormat
Paper 1Business CommunicationDescriptive
Paper 2Fundamentals of AccountingDescriptive
Paper 3Economic and Business EnvironmentDescriptive
Paper 4Business Laws and ManagementMCQ / OMR

Pass criteria: minimum 40% in each paper and 50% aggregate across all four papers, in one sitting — there is no carry-forward between attempts under the current CSEET pattern.

Level 2 — CS Executive

7 papers · 2 modules
ModulePaperSubject
Module 1Paper 1Jurisprudence, Interpretation & General Laws
Paper 2Company Law & Practice
Paper 3Setting up of Business, Industrial & Labour Laws
Paper 4Corporate Accounting and Financial Management
Module 2Paper 5Capital Market & Securities Laws
Paper 6Economic, Commercial & Intellectual Property Laws
Paper 7Tax Laws & Practice (Direct & Indirect Tax)

Pass criteria: minimum 40% in each paper and 50% aggregate within each module. Candidates who score 60%+ in a paper but fail the module get an exemption for that paper in their next attempt.

Level 3 — CS Professional

7 papers · 2 groups incl. electives
GroupFocus area
Group 1Governance, risk management, compliance & ethics; Drafting, pleadings & appearances; Secretarial audit, compliance management & due diligence; one elective (e.g. CSR & Social Governance, Internal & Forensic Audit)
Group 2Strategic management & corporate finance; Corporate restructuring, valuation & insolvency; one elective (e.g. GST & Corporate Tax Planning, Labour Laws & Practice, Banking & Insurance)

Pass criteria: same 40% per paper / 50% aggregate rule per group. Elective options change periodically — confirm the current list on icsi.edu when you register.

Indicative figures

CS salary in India

Reported ranges vary quite a bit by source — these are broad, commonly cited figures, not guarantees.

Fresher₹4–8 LPA

Listed companies and Big 4-adjacent firms pay toward the higher end.

Mid-career (4–8 yrs)₹10–20 LPA

Higher in BFSI, listed companies and firms with SEBI-heavy work.

Senior / Compliance Head₹30–60 LPA+

KMPs in large listed companies occasionally cross ₹1 crore.

Pairing CS with CA (Inter), LLB or an MBA in Finance tends to push starting and mid-career pay noticeably higher, particularly in financial services and law-adjacent roles.

Honest take

Advantages and disadvantages

Advantages

  • Guaranteed statutory demand — most listed companies must appoint a CS.
  • Lower total cost than CA or most professional degrees.
  • Can be pursued alongside a regular degree.

Disadvantages

  • Starting salaries are generally lower than CA or CFA at the fresher stage.
  • Law-heavy, descriptive papers demand strong writing and drafting skills.
  • Career growth into top pay brackets takes longer without an add-on qualification.
