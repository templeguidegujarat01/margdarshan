Plan ahead

A simple 12-month application plan

1Months 1-3

Shortlist & research

Pick countries, courses and 8 to 12 universities across safe, target and reach tiers; check fees, tests and deadlines.

2Months 3-6

Tests & documents

Prepare for and take IELTS/TOEFL and SAT/GRE/GMAT as needed; collect transcripts and recommendation letters.

3Months 6-9

Applications & essays

Write a clear statement of purpose or personal statement, submit applications and apply for scholarships.

4Months 9-11

Offers & funding

Compare offers, secure funding or an education loan and accept one offer by the deadline.

5Months 10-12

Visa & travel

Apply for the student visa with proof of funds, book accommodation and plan travel and insurance.

6Always

Beware of shortcuts

Use official university and government websites; never pay anyone who promises guaranteed admission or visa approval.

Frequently asked questions

Most public universities in Germany charge no tuition fees, including for international students, but you still pay a small semester contribution and need to cover living costs and show proof of funds. Some states charge tuition to non-EU students, so check the specific university.
Both are widely accepted. IELTS is popular for the UK, Canada, Australia and Ireland, and TOEFL is common for the USA, but most universities accept either. Check your target universities' requirements and choose the test whose format suits you.
Many master's programmes have made GRE optional, but some still require it, especially in the USA. The GMAT is used for MBA and business programmes. Always check the programme page for the current policy.
It is a visa or permit that lets graduates stay and work for a limited period after finishing their degree. Examples include the US OPT, the UK Graduate Route, the Canadian PGWP, the Australian Temporary Graduate visa, Ireland's stay-back scheme and Germany's 18-month job-seeker permit.
Options include scholarships such as Fulbright, Chevening and DAAD, university merit awards, assistantships for master's and PhD students, education loans, and family savings. Most countries also require proof of funds for the visa.

*Costs, visa rules and test formats are indicative and change frequently. Confirm everything with the official university, government and test-body websites.
