AFCAT — Air Force Common Admission Test

15 min read ·Conducted by the Indian Air Force ·For graduates and engineers

Air Force Common Admission Test (AFCAT) is the Indian Air Force's own exam for selecting commissioned officers into the Flying, Ground Duty (Technical) and Ground Duty (Non-Technical) branches. It is held twice a year, is open to both men and women, and leads straight to officer training at the Air Force Academy — no Union Public Service Commission (UPSC) exam involved.

Overview

What is AFCAT?

  • The AFCAT is conducted by the Indian Air Force to recruit officers through Short Service Commission (SSC) in the Flying branch and through Permanent or Short Service Commission in the Ground Duty branches.
  • Candidates who clear the online test are called to an Air Force Selection Board (AFSB) for a five-day assessment, followed by a medical examination.
  • Selected candidates train at the Air Force Academy, Dundigal (Hyderabad) and are commissioned as Flying Officers.
Read moreRead less↓
  • Ground Duty (Technical) cadets also take an Engineering Knowledge Test (EKT) along with AFCAT.
  • National Cadet Corps (NCC) Air Wing Senior Division 'C' certificate holders can enter the Flying branch through the NCC Special Entry without writing AFCAT.
Fit check

Who should choose AFCAT

✔ Ideal for you if…

  • Want an officer's career in the Air Force and are a graduate or engineer.
  • Have strong numerical and reasoning skills and can think quickly under time pressure.
  • Are physically fit, comfortable with discipline and open to postings anywhere in India.

✖ Reconsider if…

  • Did not study Physics and Maths in Class 12 but want the Flying branch — it is compulsory for pilots.
  • Want a fixed home-city job; Air Force officers are posted across bases and transfer regularly.
  • Are not prepared for the AFSB's multi-day psychological and group testing.

What gives you an edge

Science (PCM) in Class 12

Mandatory for the Flying branch and Ground Duty (Technical); Maths helps in the Numerical Ability section.

Engineering graduates

Natural fit for Aeronautical Engineering (Electronics / Mechanical) branches via EKT.

Commerce & Arts graduates

Eligible for Ground Duty (Non-Technical) — Administration, Logistics, Accounts (B.Com) and Education.

Step-by-step plan for your background

Arts / Humanities

If you're from Arts

  1. Arts graduates with 60% can join Ground Duty (Non-Technical) — Administration, Logistics or Education (with a Postgraduate (PG)).
  2. Build Numerical Ability from Class 10 basics; it is the main hurdle for most Arts students.
  3. Practise Officer Intelligence Rating tests and group discussions for AFSB.
Commerce

If you're from Commerce

  1. B.Com graduates with 60% are eligible for the Accounts branch, plus Administration and Logistics.
  2. Use your arithmetic strength in Numerical Ability; work on Reasoning & Military Aptitude.
  3. Prepare for AFSB group tasks and the interview.
Science

If you're from Science

  1. With Physics and Maths in Class 12 you can target the Flying branch; engineers can also take EKT for Technical branches.
  2. Practise for the Computerised Pilot Selection System (CPSS) if you choose Flying.
  3. Check eyesight standards early before investing months of preparation.

Step-by-step

AFCAT selection process

Stage 1

Apply online

Register on the AFCAT portal during the notification window and choose your branch preferences.

Stage 2

AFCAT online test

A 2-hour objective test (plus EKT for Ground Duty Technical). Shortlisting is on merit against the AFCAT cut-off.

Stage 3

AFSB — Stage I

Officer Intelligence Rating test and Picture Perception & Discussion Test. Candidates who do not clear Stage I return the same day.

Stage 4

AFSB — Stage II

Psychological tests, Group Testing Officer tasks and a personal interview over the next few days. Flying candidates also take the Computerised Pilot Selection System (CPSS).

Stage 5

Medical examination

Detailed medical tests at an Air Force medical establishment.

Stage 6

Merit list & training

An all-India merit list by branch and vacancies; selected cadets join the Air Force Academy, Dundigal.

Key rules at a glance

Marking

+3 for every correct answer in AFCAT and EKT.

Negative marking

−1 for every wrong answer; unanswered questions carry no penalty.

Mode & language

Online, computer-based, in English only.

Exam fee

A fee is charged for AFCAT; NCC Special Entry candidates do not pay. Check the notification for the current amount.

Pay, perks & work

AFCAT salary, designation & job profile

IAF officers are paid on the 7th Central Pay Commission defence pay matrix, plus Military Service Pay and branch-specific allowances.

Pay Level 10 on commission, with annual increments.

Job role & responsibilities

Flying Branch

Fly fighter, transport or helicopter aircraft after specialised flying training.

Ground Duty — Technical

Maintain aircraft, avionics, weapons and radar systems as an aeronautical engineer.

Ground Duty — Non-Technical

Run administration, logistics, accounts, meteorology or education functions at Air Force stations.

Perks

Free medical care, subsidised accommodation and canteen, leave travel, group insurance and pension (for permanent commission).

AFCAT-specific tips

Master the basics of Maths

Numerical Ability is Class 10 arithmetic — speed comes from daily practice.

Read defence news

General Awareness regularly includes questions on defence exercises, aircraft and appointments.

Practise mock tests

With −1 for wrong answers, accuracy matters more than attempting every question.

Prepare for AFSB early

Practise communication, group discussion and Officer Intelligence Rating tests alongside the written exam.
